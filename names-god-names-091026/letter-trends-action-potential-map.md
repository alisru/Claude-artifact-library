# Single-Letter Trends on the Relative Action-Potential Map

## The map

Four corners and a centre. Productive (can be), reductive (is not), regress (was like), constructive (is like), and "is" in the middle.

The loop runs clockwise along the edges: productive to reductive (top, latent active), reductive to regress (right, exclusive), regress to constructive (bottom, latent counteractive), constructive back up to productive (left, inclusive).

The two diagonals pass through "is" and join opposite corners: can be with was like, and is not with is like.

For each letter below, "feeds into" means the next corner along the loop, and "opposite" means the diagonal partner. A letter at the centre connects to all four corners.

## Data used

The 105 two-letter entries in the bigram table, including the nine added in the God-names run. For each letter the recurring meaning was read from the entry cluster lines, separately for the letter as first and as second member of the bigram.

## Bigrams that sit on the edges and diagonals

These are bigrams where one sound holds two meanings, and the two meanings fall on adjacent or opposite corners of the map.

| Place on the map | Bigrams | Why |
|---|---|---|

## Patterns across letters

Consonants carry the kind of force, and final vowels carry where it points. The strong consonants (L flow, M measure and hold, N not and within, S being, T placing, H breath) each hold one kind of action, and the strong final vowels point: A toward, E at the self or the this-one, I at me, O along the path.

Several letters change meaning with position, and the change follows the map. B and C swing across a diagonal, B between entering and departing and C between together and cutting. M slides along the left edge from measure to hold. N crosses the other diagonal from not to within. L, T and H hold the same meaning in either position.

The corpus keeps finding one sound with two opposite meanings, and the map locates where those opposites sit: on the edges for adjacent corners and on the diagonals for opposite ones.

Initial vowels do not carry a single trend. The meaning lives in the final vowel and in the consonants.
