# Alphabet of Basic Forms

Each letter has one meaning read from its shape, projected across the seven planes, and mapped to the Hegemony as a (υ, ψ) coordinate. Letters are built from other letters, so composite meanings and composite coordinates compound. This is the pre-bigram table: from basic form up to Action-Effect.

Continues the Actualism document (The Lyrical Plane of Meaning). Its four perspectives are kept. Its Hegemony values are recalculated, because it scored on 0 to 1 and the Hegemony runs −2 to +2 on both axes, so the original numbers could not reach the negative half of either axis and every letter came out in the productive quadrant.

---

## The principle

A letter's meaning is a word beginning with that letter whose sense is what the shape does. A is activity: two streams spaced apart converging, with the bar marking activity over a span of time, potential converging to a point and expanding from one. C is container: the open bowl. I is ice: the wall that does not move. E is energy: the wall reaching out.

Composite letters are made of other letters. B is boundary because it is two D's stacked and opposing on the wall of I, which is energy on the wall of ice. D is direction because it is a C closed against I, a container given one fixed side so it can only open from the other.

I and C are the two irreducibles. I rotated gives the bar and the diagonal stream. C rotated gives the four bowls. Everything else composes from those and from each other.

---

## Reading the Hegemony from a form

υ, morality, asks who the form's function serves. A form that distributes outward to everything scores positive. A form that holds inward to itself scores negative. The scale is the standard one: +2 everyone, +1 others, 0 no one, −1 my group only, −2 only me.

ψ, will, asks what the form's energy is doing. A form that builds, reaches or acts scores positive. A form that withholds, encloses or consumes scores negative. +2 actively creating, +1 proactive, 0 neutral, −1 passive or withholding, −2 actively extracting.

Each of the four perspectives gives its own coordinate, because a form read from the top is not doing what the same form is doing read from the base. The resultant is the mean of the four and is the letter's standing position.

These are first-pass values. They are testable: a letter's resultant should sit where its meaning sits, and where it does not, either the value or the meaning is wrong.

## How this feeds Action-Effect

A letter has a meaning and a coordinate. A composite letter compounds both from the letters it is built of. A bigram is two letters adjacent, so it has a first-pass meaning and a first-pass coordinate from the letters alone, before any citation is consulted. A word is a chain of bigrams. The Action-Effect reading is built from the form upward, and the word's coordinate is the compound of its letters, checkable against the coordinate the named act would score on its own.

The Omni-Weave bigram table stays a separate instrument, since its meanings are attested across languages rather than read from shape. Where a bigram's form-derived reading and its citation-derived reading agree, that is two methods converging. Where they disagree, both are held.

## Open

The twenty proposed meanings are proposed. Each is testable: the word must begin with the letter and its sense must be what the shape does. A better word meeting both tests replaces the proposal.

The coordinates are first-pass. The test is whether a letter's resultant sits where its meaning sits. Two already pass that test in a useful way: E and F differ only by the ground and the coordinate separates them on υ, and P and R differ only by the leg and the υ flips sign across it.
