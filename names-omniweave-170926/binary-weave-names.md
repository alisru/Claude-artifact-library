The name runs a complete person-cycle: it opens in the distant third person (WI apart-above, IL that/he/god), contracts to the first person singular (LI to-me), releases back into the third (IA he/she/it), reopens as first person collective (AM I-am and people), and terminates plural (MS). What is raised and shut is raised and shut in order to be poured somewhere specific, and the somewhere is a people that will be counted.

**Orthodox**
Patronymic surname, son of William. William is from Proto-Germanic *Wiljahelmaz, a compound of *wiljo = will/wish/desire and *helmaz = helm/helmet/protective covering, the latter from PIE *kel- = to cover/conceal. Reached English via Old Norman Williame, a back-borrowing from Medieval Latin Willelmus; the expected native form *Wilhelm is unattested in written English at any period. After the Conquest the most popular given name in England until displaced by John. The terminal -s is the English patronymic genitive.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: will plus helm -- desire under a protective covering, then the patronymic -s.
Weave: a separation-from-above raising the divine-and-distal to sealed completion, poured inward, settled as condition and other, fastened into a loving common-paternal collective, counted as many.

The helm converges exactly, and the full hold sharpens it past what the orthodox gloss states. Sumerian il = to raise/carry into LL (the lateral doubled to completion) is a covering lifted and sealed at full extent, which is a helmet by function rather than description. LL+LI then supplies the part the orthodox leaves implicit: the sealing exists so the contents can be aimed. A helm is not a lid, it is a closure that directs what it encloses back at the one enclosed.

The patronymic converges on the terminal run, and the strongest single citation is one that was previously discarded. Arabic amm = paternal uncle, and by extension public and common, sits immediately before the plural coda MS. A patronymic surname is precisely the paternal made common and then made countable. AM also holds people-nation, I-am, love and binding; the whole slice is the operation of turning one father into a shared, counted many. IA sits before it running the same conversion on place rather than person -- the Greek and Latin land-of suffix.

The will does not converge. WI attests as *wi = apart/separation and Korean wi = above, with no citation carrying desire or volition anywhere in the slice. The orthodox *wiljo has no phonetic support in the cascade. Held as divergence, and noted against Travis, where Sanskrit iS = to desire does appear.

A second divergence is worth holding in the other direction. IL carries a substantial divine load -- Hebrew il as archaic El, Arabic il- as god-directional -- and the orthodox etymology contains nothing divine at all. The weave produces more than the orthodox here, not less.

### Travis Williams -- Combined Collapse

Travis: an exposing halt that is itself the beam laid across, yielding everything it arrests to a moving origin reachable only through a divide that hardens into a wanting third-person body. The mover become the fixture.
Williams: a separation-from-above raising the divine-and-distal to sealed completion, poured inward to the first person, settled as condition and other, fastened into a collective that says I-am and names its binding love and its origin common-paternal, then counted as many.

**Combined**
One who stopped inside the divide and became it -- exposing, watching, wounding and giving to everything that passes -- while carrying a strength that was raised, sealed shut, and thereby aimed inward, which settles as place and as other, fastens into a people that calls its fastening love and its father common, and is finally counted out as many. Read end to end: the fixture in the gap holds a closed thing whose closure is what lets it be directed, and it directs it into a countable line.

Both names run the same operation on separation and both resolve it the same way. Travis divides with VI (force that is apartness) and Williams with WI (apartness that is aboveness), one lateral and one vertical. In both, the separation is not an obstacle to reaching across it but the precondition: Travis reaches origin only through the force that made origin distant, and Williams can only pour toward what it first stood apart from and above.

Both also run a fixed-versus-flowing paradox and resolve it by the same rule. Travis: the bird becomes the beam, the flight becomes the ice and sinew. Williams: the seal is what makes the pour directional. Motion in this pair is never free motion. It is motion that has been arrested into a structure, and the arrest is what gives it an address.

### 7-Plane Projection

**Q1 WHO -- Metaphysical**
A third person before it is a first person. Both names open in the distal -- Travis in Latin is = he/him standing in the divide, Williams in Latin ille = that and French il = he/it above and apart. Will and direction here belong to something pointed at rather than something speaking, and the only first-person moment in the whole pair is LI (to-me), which arrives already as the recipient of a pour rather than as an initiator.

**Q2 WHAT -- Possible**
Harm and gift held unresolved (RA), light and bareness as one act (RA), sealed and pouring at once (LL against LI), apart and toward in one slice (WI against Latin il-), and the god held indistinguishably from a mere pronoun (IL). The potential of this pair is that every one of its states reads as its own opposite depending on the side approached from, and none of the pairs collapse.

**Q3 WHERE -- Physical**
In the divide, and above it. Travis places its body inside the separation and freezes it there (VI into IS as ice and sinew), with the far bank named by Italian vi = there. Williams places its operation over the top (Korean wi = above) and pours down. Together: a position occupied in the gap and a position occupied over it, which are the two places a transverse beam touches.

**Q4 WHY -- Lyrical**
Because exposure is the only thing a halt can actually do -- light, uncovering and watching being one operation in RA -- and whether that exposure lands as wound or as gift is not the halt's to decide. And because a strength raised and sealed (IL+LL) that is never turned inward (LI) has no address; the closure exists to make the aim possible, not to protect the contents.

**Q5 HOW -- Logical**
By stopping and releasing (TR); by exposing, watching, wounding, giving and lying across (RA); by handing to the begetter who flies (AV); by dividing and locating the far side (VI); by freezing into a wanting third-person ligament (IS). Then by standing apart and above (WI); by lifting the divine and the distant (IL); by sealing at full extent (LL); by pouring inward at me (LI); by settling as condition, land and other (IA); by fastening into a people, a love and a common father (AM); by counting it out (MS).

**Q6 CAUSE -- Historical**
The priors are traffic and inheritance, both already underway. TR requires something already moving before a stop can be set in it; AM+MS requires a father already in place before the paternal can be made common and counted. Neither name begins anything. Travis operates on what was already crossing; Williams operates on what was already held.

**Q7 EFFECT -- Emotive**
The pair produces the specific feeling of an occupied position rather than a journey or a construction. One is stopped in the gap, exposed to everything that passes and exposing it in turn, holding something sealed that can only be aimed because it was shut. The felt result is not ownership and not passage but being the address that others are measured against -- and, in the terminal MS, the knowledge that the position will be counted out to successors whether or not they wanted it.

## Lamech, Adah and Zillah (Genesis 4:19)

Run as a set. Lamech and his two wives are the first named triad of its kind in the text, and the three names turn out to share and withhold bigrams in a way that is itself the finding.

---

### Lamech (לֶמֶךְ)

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
LAMECH -> LA . AM . ME . EC . CH

LA: Arabic la = no/not [100%] . French/Spanish/Italian la = the, feminine definite [100%] . Hawaiian la = sun/day [100%] . Archaic English la = look!/see!/behold! [100%] . Solfege la = sixth note of the scale [100%]
AM: Hebrew am = people/nation [100%] . English am = 1st person singular to-be [100%] . Arabic amm = paternal uncle/public/common [100%] . Latin amare = to love [100%] . Sanskrit am = to bind/fasten [90%]
ME: Latin me = me, accusative/oblique [100%] . French me = me/to-me [100%] . Hebrew me = from/out-of [100%] . PIE *me = first person oblique [100%]
EC: Latin ec- = prefix on interrogatives carrying intensive or indefinite force [90%] . structural: the vowel arrested by the hard velar stop
CH: Hebrew chai = life [100%] . Scottish/Irish loch = lake/water-body [100%] . Sanskrit candra = moon [90%]

**Layer 1**
LA + AM: negation, the feminine definite, sun-day, behold and the sixth note meeting people-nation, I-am, the paternal-public-common, love and binding. The refusal-that-is-also-a-summons landing on a collective that says I-am: a light cast over a people, a father held in common, a love and a fastening, with the negation sitting in the same slice as the love it would deny. The definite article meets the nation it would define; the sun meets those who live under it.

AM + ME: people, I-am, the paternal-common, love and binding meeting me, to-me, from-me and the first-person oblique. The nation and the I-am contracting to a single first person that is simultaneously object and source. The collective and its common father narrow to one man who is both what things are done to and where things come from.

ME + EC: me, to-me, from-me and the first-person oblique meeting the intensifying-indefinite force that attaches to questions. The first person made emphatic and indeterminate at once -- the self converted into the pressure behind a question rather than the content of an answer.

EC + CH: the intensifying-indefinite interrogative force meeting life, the lake-water-body and the moon. Maximum unmeasured emphasis arriving at what breathes, what is wet, and what shines with light it did not make.

**Layer 2**
(LA+AM) + (AM+ME): the beholding-refusal over the people, the common father and the love, contracting to the first person that is both object and origin. The selective light over a nation narrowing until it is one man saying me and out-of-me.

(AM+ME) + (ME+EC): the nation-love-binding narrowed to one first person, then intensified into indefinite question. The collective becomes a man, and the man becomes an emphasis with nothing fixed to land on.

(ME+EC) + (EC+CH): the intensified first-person question arriving at life, water and moon. The emphatic indeterminate self coming down on the living thing in the dark.

**Layer 3**
The beholding-refusal over a people that says I-am and calls its binding love, contracting to one first person who is both object and source, intensified into an indefinite maximum, and arriving at life in the water and under the moon.

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a selective light -- behold and refusal being a single gesture, since to point at a thing is to exclude what surrounds it and to shine on a thing is to cast the rest into shadow -- laid over a people that says I-am and names its binding love and its father common; contracting to one first person who is at once the source things come from and the object things are done to, and cannot separate the two; intensified into an indefinite interrogative, which is maximum force with no determinate target; and arriving finally at life, water and the moon -- the living thing in the dark, wet, under a borrowed light.

The name resolves nothing about proportion, and that is the resolution. EC supplies total intensity and withholds the object. Whatever CH delivers it to receives the whole of a force that was never measured against anything, because there was never anything for it to be measured against.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Lemek, Strong's H3929, from an unused root of uncertain meaning. The lexicons decline to fix it. Traditional glosses offered without root support: powerful; a strong youth; and Easton's the strikerdown, the wild man. Fifth in descent from Cain, son of Methushael, the first polygamist named in the text, and the speaker of the sword-song of Genesis 4:23-24, which claims a man killed for a wound and seventy-sevenfold vengeance against Cain's sevenfold.

**Convergence**
This is the rare case where there is no orthodox etymology to converge with. The root is recorded as unused and uncertain, so the weave has no anchor and must either stand alone or converge with the text instead.

It converges with the text, closely. The sword-song is a boast structured entirely as disproportion, and EC is disproportion as a phoneme: intensive and indefinite in the same unit, maximum pressure with no fixed object. ME is the grammar of the song itself -- the wound and the killing collapse into one first person who cannot state what was done to him except in the same clause as what he did. LA+AM is the address: he calls the women who are his by name and demands hearing, which is the selective light exactly, summoning and excluding in one move. And the traditional glosses that the lexicons could not root -- powerful, strikerdown, wild man -- are descriptions of EC+CH from outside: unmeasured intensity arriving at the living.

The glosses are not etymology. They are readers describing the same structure the weave produces, having no root to work from either.

### Adah (עָדָה)

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
ADAH -> AD . DA . AH

AD: Hebrew ad = until/eternity/perpetuity [100%] . Latin ad = toward/at [100%] . PIE *ad = at/near [100%] . Old Norse at = to/toward [90%] . Welsh at = to/toward [90%] . Sanskrit adhi = upon/over/toward [85%]
DA: Sanskrit da = to give [100%] . Latin dat = he gives [100%] . Greek didomi, da- form in the aorist [90%] . Arabic da = to call/summon [100%] . Hebrew da = this/here [100%]
AH: Hebrew ah = brother [100%] . Arabic ah = sigh/release [100%] . Sanskrit ah = to say/speak [100%] . German ach = oh/alas [90%] . Yoruba ah = exclamation of surprise [100%] . Bengali ah = sigh/oh [100%] . raw open vowel = unobstructed breath, maximum aperture [inherent]

**Layer 1**
AD + DA: until-eternity-perpetuity, toward-at, near, and upon-over meeting giving, he-gives, calling-summoning, and this-here. The approach that is also forever meeting bestowal and summons, landing on this, here. Perpetuity that gives, and gives now; the direction that never terminates handing something over at a specific point.

DA + AH: giving, he-gives, calling-summoning and this-here meeting brother, the sigh-release, speech, alas, surprise, and the raw unobstructed breath at maximum aperture. The gift and the call releasing into open breath -- the summons answered by the exhale, the bestowal completing as kinship and as speech, saying and sighing in the same slice.

**Layer 2**
(AD+DA) + (DA+AH): the toward-forever that bestows and calls, releasing into kinship, speech and total aperture. What approaches perpetually and gives, gives until it is completely open.

**Layer 3**
The perpetual approach that gives and summons, opening at the end into unobstructed breath, kinship and speech.

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by an approach that does not stop -- toward and forever being the same slice -- arriving as bestowal and as summons, pointing at this and here, and releasing into the maximum open aperture: brother, sigh, speech, the breath with nothing in its way.

Adah is three slices and every one of them is outward. Toward, give, open. There is no seal in the cascade, no stop and no negation anywhere in it. The only tension available is AD's held perpetuity against AH's total release, and it resolves as duration expressed as openness: the thing that continues forever does so by never closing.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Adah, from the root adah -- glossed as ornament, adornment, beauty; the same root also carries to pass on, to advance. Mother of Jabal, father of tent-dwellers and keepers of livestock, and of Jubal, father of all who handle the harp and pipe (Genesis 4:20-21). The name recurs for the Hittite wife of Esau in Genesis 36.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: adornment, and secondarily to pass on / advance.
Weave: the perpetual approach that gives, summons, and opens completely.

Both orthodox senses converge and they converge on different slices. The advance sense is AD unmodified -- toward and forever in one unit is advance without terminus. The adornment sense converges through DA+AH: an ornament is a bestowal (DA, to give) completed as open display (AH, maximum aperture, nothing withheld). It is a gift worn where it can be seen. The cascade contains no concealment slice at all, which is structurally what adornment is -- the opposite of covering.

The children split the cascade cleanly, which is the stronger finding. Jabal takes AD: tents and livestock are the perpetual approach, dwelling that moves and never settles. Jubal takes DA+AH: the harp and the pipe are the call (DA, to summon) completing as unobstructed breath (AH), and a pipe is literally a column of air with nothing in its way. Adah's two slices become her two sons, one each.

### Zillah (צִלָּה)

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
ZILLAH -> ZI . IL . LL . LA . AH

ZI: Chinese zi = child/seed/son [100%] . Chinese zi = written character/word [100%] . Chinese zi = self/own [100%] . Japanese ji = character/ground/self [100%]
IL: Hebrew il = strength/power, archaic form of El [100%] . Arabic il- = god/toward-divine [100%] . Sumerian il = to raise/carry [100%] . Latin il- = in/toward [100%] . French/Italian il = he/it [100%] . Latin ille = that, distal demonstrative [100%]
LL: structural doubling of the lateral -- the flowing continuant held to completion, sealed at its full extent; Welsh ll is a distinct voiceless lateral fricative phoneme
LA: Arabic la = no/not [100%] . French/Spanish/Italian la = the, feminine definite [100%] . Hawaiian la = sun/day [100%] . Archaic English la = look!/see!/behold! [100%] . Solfege la = sixth note [100%]
AH: Hebrew ah = brother [100%] . Arabic ah = sigh/release [100%] . Sanskrit ah = to say/speak [100%] . German ach = oh/alas [90%] . Yoruba ah = exclamation of surprise [100%] . Bengali ah = sigh/oh [100%] . raw open vowel = unobstructed breath, maximum aperture [inherent]

**Layer 1**
ZI + IL: the seed-child-son that is already a written character and already a self, meeting divine strength, the god, the raising-carrying, the toward-into, the he/it, and the distal that. The seed-word-self lifted toward a power that is simultaneously God and merely that thing pointed at from a distance. What is raised is a form that already contains itself; what raises it may be divine or may be only a pronoun.

IL + LL: divine strength, the god, the raising-carrying, the toward, the he/it and the distal that, doubled to completion and sealed. The lift finished and shut at full extent, no remainder -- whether what was lifted was a god or a mere he, it is now closed over.

LL + LA: the sealed completion meeting negation, the feminine definite, sun-day, behold and the sixth note. The shut and finished thing meeting a light that is also a refusal and also a pointing. What has been closed over is met by what selects and excludes.

LA + AH: negation, the feminine definite, sun-day, behold and the sixth note meeting brother, the sigh-release, speech, alas, surprise, and the unobstructed breath at maximum aperture. The selective light releasing into total openness -- the refusal-and-behold opening completely, the not completing as an exhale, the sun arriving at the aperture where it lands.

**Layer 2**
(ZI+IL) + (IL+LL): the seed-word-self raised toward the divine-or-distal, then doubled and sealed. The written character lifted and shut at full extent.

(IL+LL) + (LL+LA): the sealed lift standing in the selective light. What was raised and closed is now precisely what the sun falls on and what the pointing selects.

(LL+LA) + (LA+AH): the sealed thing in the selective light releasing into open breath. The shut-and-lit opening completely at the end, into the aperture where the result lands.

**Layer 3**
The seed that is already a word and already a self, raised toward the divine-or-merely-distal, sealed at full extent, stood in a light that shows by refusing, and released into unobstructed breath.

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a seed that is already a word and already a self, lifted toward a power that may be God or may be only a pronoun held at a distance, sealed at its full extent with no remainder, met by a light that shows by refusing -- behold and not being one gesture, since to point at a thing is to exclude what surrounds it -- and released at the last into unobstructed breath.

The sealed-yet-met paradox resolves against the aperture the name ends on, and against the other name in the verse. Adah's cascade has no seal at all, so what reaches her passes through and completes as open breath. Zillah's has one, so what reaches her is stopped at it, and the AH she also terminates in receives whatever the stopping produced rather than the light itself. The cascade does not say what that is. It says only that the same terminal opening receives a transmission in one name and a consequence in the other.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Tsillah, Strong's H6741, the feminine of tsel = shadow, from tsalal = to be dark, to be shaded. Glossed as shade, shadow, and in some lexicons protection. Mother of Tubal-Cain, forger of every tool of bronze and iron, and of Naamah (Genesis 4:22). Commentators note an Assyrian parallel, tzillatu, the shades of night, while holding that Hebrew suffices.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: shadow, shade.
Weave: a sealed seed-form raised, met by a light that is also a refusal, releasing into the open.

The convergence rests on one bigram and it is worth stating narrowly rather than broadly. Hawaiian la (sun) and Arabic la (not) occupy the same phoneme. A darkness produced by light rather than by its absence is what shade is, and LA holds both terms of that in a single unit. LL supplies a body sealed with no remainder, which is what a light must meet rather than pass through. ZI supplies a form that already contains itself. AH supplies the opening the result exits by.

That is the extent of it. The cascade produces a sealed thing, a light that excludes, and an opening. It does not produce an edge, an outline, or a cast shape -- those are the orthodox word supplying its own geometry, and naming them inside the weave would be importing the answer. What the weave genuinely contributes is that shade is an event rather than an object: a refusal and an illumination in one gesture, meeting something closed.

Tubal-Cain converges on the same two slices as his mother's centre. Working bronze and iron is IL+LL: raising -- heating, lifting -- and then sealing to completion with no remainder. The operation that makes his craft is the operation that makes her name.

Tubal-Cain converges on the same two slices as his mother's centre. Working bronze and iron is IL+LL exactly: raising -- heating, lifting -- and then sealing into a closed form with a fixed edge. He produces a shaped closed body by the same operation that produces his mother's name. She makes an outline with light; he makes one with metal.

### The Triad -- Combined Collapse

Lamech: a selective light over a people, contracted to one first person who is both source and object, intensified without measure, arriving at the living.
Adah: a perpetual approach that gives and summons and opens completely. No closure anywhere in it.
Zillah: a sealed seed-form raised, met by a light that is also a refusal, releasing into the open.

**Combined**
A man who carries a selective light and cannot measure it stands between two apertures. One never closes, and so what reaches it passes through and completes as a gift, a call, and open breath. The other closes completely, and so what reaches it is stopped, and its terminal opening receives the consequence rather than the light. He is the source of both and can distinguish neither from what is done to him.

**The bigram distribution is itself the finding**

LA appears in Lamech and in Zillah. It does not appear in Adah.
AH appears in Adah and in Zillah. It does not appear in Lamech.
Lamech and Adah share no bigram at all.

So the husband connects to the second wife through the light, the two wives connect to each other through the aperture, and there is no phonetic contact whatever between Lamech and Adah. Zillah is the only point of the triad that touches both, and she touches each through the slice the third party lacks.

Under the working hypothesis in the table -- that a terminal bigram gives the vector of a name rather than the nature of its bearer -- the two wives are aimed at the same place. Both terminate in AH, total unobstructed aperture. Lamech terminates in CH: life, water, the moon. They are aimed at the open; he is aimed at the living thing.

Adah has no seal in her cascade and Zillah's cascade is built on one. Both terminate in the same total aperture. They are the two available outcomes when something arrives at an opening: passing through it, or being stopped before it. Ornament and shadow are not opposites in this reading. They are what the same terminal opening reports depending on whether anything closed stood in front of it.

### 7-Plane Projection

**Q1 WHO -- Metaphysical**
One first person and two apertures. Lamech is the only slice-set in the triad containing ME, and it contains it as both object and source, so the will in this group is located in a man who experiences his own action as something happening to him. The women are not given first-person slices at all; their identity is structural -- what happens when something passes or does not pass.

**Q2 WHAT -- Possible**
Behold and refusal in one phoneme (LA), the god and the mere pronoun in one phoneme (IL), intensity without object (EC), the source and the object in one first person (ME). Every ambiguity in the triad is an ambiguity about whether something is maximal or arbitrary, and none of them resolve in favour of either.

**Q3 WHERE -- Physical**
The triad has almost no ordinary location in it. Lamech holds the light, Adah is an opening with nothing closed in it, Zillah is a sealed thing that light meets. Those are positions relative to each other rather than places. The only element with a location in the ordinary sense is CH -- the water and the moonlight Lamech's force finally lands in.

**Q4 WHY -- Lyrical**
Because a light that selects must exclude, and something has to receive the exclusion. Adah takes the transmission and Zillah takes the occlusion, and neither is a choice either of them makes. And because EC cannot be satisfied: an intensity with no determinate object will keep going until it meets something living.

**Q5 HOW -- Logical**
By pointing and refusing at once (LA), narrowing a people to a man (AM into ME), intensifying without target (EC), and landing on the living (CH). Then, on one side, by approaching without end, giving, calling and opening (AD, DA, AH). On the other, by raising a self-containing form (ZI, IL), sealing it to completion (LL), meeting it with the selective light (LA), and releasing into the aperture (AH).

**Q6 CAUSE -- Historical**
The priors are a people and a light. AM requires a nation already assembled before it can be narrowed to one man; LA requires something already there to be pointed at before it can exclude the rest. Neither wife's cascade initiates anything either -- Adah receives an approach, Zillah receives a lift and a light. The only element in the triad that applies force is EC, and it applies it without having been given anything to apply it to.

**Q7 EFFECT -- Emotive**
The triad produces the specific discomfort of watching an unmeasured force stand between two things that cannot refuse it. The felt result is not violence but exposure: one figure holds a light that shows by excluding, and the other two are defined entirely by what that light does when it reaches them. The text gives both women speech only as an audience -- hear my voice -- and the cascade agrees: both of them terminate in an aperture, which is a structure for receiving.

## Achamoth, Chakhmath, Sophia and Sophiea -- Four Renderings

Run as a set. These are four spellings pointing at one referent across two traditions, and the point of running all four is that the cascades are not the same. Where the spellings diverge, the weave diverges with them, and the divergence is the finding.

AM + MO: people, I-am, the paternal-common, love and binding meeting more-greater, also-even-too, measure-manner and measure itself. The collective and its love arriving at measure and at excess in one slice: the nation counted, and the nation exceeded.

MO + OT: more-greater, also, measure-manner and measure meeting the sign that marks an agreement between two parties, the wondrous sign, the grass-herb and the then-therefore. Measure and excess arriving at a token held between two, at ground-cover, and at consequence. What is counted becomes what is marked between.

OT + TH: the two-party token, the wonder-sign, the grass and the therefore meeting the death-marker, the placing-setting and the demonstrative pointing. The token set down and pointed at -- the sign placed, and the placing that is also a death-notation.

**Layer 2**
(AC+CH) + (CH+HA): the sharp restriction entering the living water, which meets its own negation and departs. The needle in the wet thing and the "but" that removes it.
(CH+HA) + (HA+AM): the negated-departed living thing arriving at the people that says I-am and fastens itself in love.
(HA+AM) + (AM+MO): the departed-and-definite collective arriving at measure and at more-than-measure.
(AM+MO) + (MO+OT): the measured-and-exceeded collective arriving at a token held between two parties.
(MO+OT) + (OT+TH): the token of agreement set down, pointed at, and marked.

**Layer 3**
The sharpening restriction entering the living water, negating and departing it, arriving at a people that binds itself in love, measured and then exceeded, coming to rest as a two-party token set down and pointed at.

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a sharpening that is also a restriction -- the needle and the "but" being one gesture, since to qualify is to cut -- driven into the living water; which negates and departs what it entered while the mouth stays open; arriving at a people that says I-am and fastens itself in love; measured and then exceeded, measure and more-than occupying the same slice; and coming to rest as a token of agreement between two parties, set down and pointed at, the setting-down being also a mark of death.

The paradoxes resolve into one another. AC's point and AC's "but" are the same operation at different scales, since to qualify a statement is to cut it. MO's measure and MO's more-than are the same pairing repeated: to measure is to draw a line, and to exceed is to be what the line failed to contain. And OT closes it, because a token marking an agreement between two parties is exactly what a thing needs once it has been cut -- something must hold the halves in relation. The name's whole motion is the manufacture of that token. The cut at the front and the two-party mark at the back are one event.

**Orthodox**
Greek Achamoth, a transliteration rather than a Greek formation, derived from the Hebrew chokmah, and specifically from the plural form chokmoth. In Valentinian cosmology as reported by Irenaeus the term names the lower of two, distinguished from the upper Sophia; the standard scholarly treatment pairs them as upper and lower under the same word. The name is therefore not an independent term but a marked variant of another.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: a transliterated Hebrew plural used to name the lower of a pair.
Weave: a cut driven into the living water, arriving at a token of agreement held between two parties, set down.

Because the name is a transliteration it has no root of its own, so there is nothing etymological to converge against. What there is instead is a function, and the weave converges on the function precisely. The word exists in order to mark a division inside something that was previously spoken of as one. AC opens the cascade with a cut that is simultaneously a "but" -- a qualification -- and OT closes it with a mark that stands for a relation between two parties rather than for a thing. The name is a divided thing's holding-mark, which is exactly the role the term plays in the system that uses it: it exists because the referent had to be spoken of as two.

The morphology agrees from the other direction. The OT the weave reads as a two-party token is, in the Hebrew, the plural ending -oth. The slice that produces "held between two" is the slice that literally pluralises the word.

### Chakhmath (\u05d7\u05b8\u05db\u05b0\u05de\u05b8\u05d4)

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
CHAKHMATH -> CH . HA . AK . KH . HM . MA . AT . TH

CH: Hebrew chai = life [100%] . Scottish and Irish loch = lake/water-body [100%] . Sanskrit candra = moon [90%]
HA: Greek ha- = without/not [100%] . Hebrew ha = the definite article [100%] . Sanskrit ha = gone/departed [100%] . Japanese ha = tooth/leaf [100%] . Swahili ha = no/not [100%] . Mandarin ha = laughing sound/open [100%]
AK: Turkish, Gagauz and Turkmen ak = white, figuratively clean and honest [100%] . Turkish ak = second-person imperative of akmak, to flow [100%] . Old Saxon and Gothic ak = but [100%] . Antillean Creole ak = and/with [100%] . Veps ak = old woman [90%]
KH: structural -- the voiceless velar or uvular fricative rendered as a digraph. No standalone semantic citation found. The scraping breath at the back of the mouth, friction rather than stop.
HM: sound-symbolic -- the closed-mouth nasal hum, breath held with the mouth shut. Structurally the inverse of AH.
MA: Hebrew ma = what/how [100%] . Sanskrit ma = measure/not/mother [100%] . Japanese ma = space/interval/pause [100%] . PIE *meh2 = to measure [100%]
AT: English at = position/location [100%] . PIE *h2ed = at/near [100%] . Old Norse at [100%] . Gothic at [100%]
TH: Greek theta = death-marker [100%] . Greek tithenai = to place/set [100%] . Old English demonstratives [100%] . Old Norse thorn [100%]

**Layer 1**
CH + HA: life, water-body and moon meeting without-not, the definite article, the gone-departed, the tooth-leaf and the open mouth. The living wet thing meeting negation and definiteness together, and the mouth opening.

HA + AK: the negated, the definite, the departed, the tooth and the open mouth meeting white, clean and honest, the imperative to flow, the "but", the and-with, and the old woman. The departed known thing arriving at whiteness and at a command to flow, with the adversative sitting in the same slice as the command.

AK + KH: white, clean and honest, the flowing imperative, the "but", the and-with and the old woman meeting the scraping fricative at the back of the mouth. The white flowing thing arrested by friction.

KH + HM: the scraping breath meeting the closed-mouth hum of held consideration. Friction passing into a sealed hum -- the scrape becoming a sustained sound with the mouth shut.

HM + MA: the closed hum meeting what-how, measure, not, mother, and the space-interval-pause. Breath held behind a shut mouth arriving at the question, at measure, at the mother, and at the gap that is not yet filled.

MA + AT: what-how, measure, not, mother and the pause meeting position and location. The measuring question given a where -- the interval fixed at a place.

AT + TH: position and location meeting the death-marker, the placing-setting and the demonstrative. The located thing set down and pointed at.

**Layer 2**
(CH+HA) + (HA+AK): the living water negated and departed, arriving at whiteness and the command to flow.
(HA+AK) + (AK+KH): the departed whiteness commanded to flow, then arrested by friction.
(AK+KH) + (KH+HM): the flow scraped and passed into a sealed hum.
(KH+HM) + (HM+MA): the scraped hum released as the measuring question, the mother, and the interval.
(HM+MA) + (MA+AT): the held question fixed at a position.
(MA+AT) + (AT+TH): the positioned question set down and pointed at.

**Layer 3**
The living water negated and departed, turned white and commanded to flow, arrested by friction, held as a hum behind a shut mouth, released as the measuring question, fixed at a position, and set down.

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by living water that meets its own negation, is turned white and told to flow, is arrested by a friction at the back of the mouth, is held as a hum with the mouth shut, and is released as the measuring question -- what, how, and the pause that is not yet an answer -- then fixed at a position and set down as a mark.

The name carries its own contradiction in the middle and resolves it there. AK is the imperative to flow. KH is obstruction. HM is closure. Three consecutive slices are current, friction and seal, and what emerges immediately after them is MA, the question. A question is what a flow becomes when it is obstructed and then held. The measuring question in this name is not where it starts. It is the product of interrupting a current and keeping the mouth shut about it.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew chokmah, Strong's H2451, from the root chet-kaf-mem; the adjective chakam means wise or skilful. The noun occurs 149 times in the Masoretic Text and is cognate with Arabic hikma. Rendered sophia in the Septuagint and sapientia in the Vulgate. Beyond the abstract sense the lexicons record technical expertise and craftsmanship as a primary usage -- Bezalel and Oholiab are filled with chokmah for the work of the tabernacle. The Zohar at Numbers 220b reads the word as a compound in two ways: koach mah, the power of mah, and cheich mah, the palate of mah.

**Convergence -- Striking**
Orthodox: the root chet-kaf-mem, plus the Zohar's two internal readings, koach mah and cheich mah.
Weave: living water negated, turned white and told to flow, scraped, sealed, released as the measuring question, fixed and set down.

The Zohar convergence is exact and it is on the two slices the weave found load-bearing. The Zohar divides the word so that mah stands as its own unit and everything before it is the thing that acts on mah. MA is precisely where the weave's cascade turns -- the question that the obstructed flow becomes. Two methods dividing the same word arrive at the same pivot, and neither needs the other to do it.

The second Zohar reading names the other one. Cheich is the palate, and KH in the weave is the scraping fricative made at the back of the mouth -- the palate is where that friction physically occurs. The Zohar reads the word as the palate of mah; the weave produces a friction at the palate immediately before mah. That is the same two-element structure identified from opposite directions, one by Hebrew wordplay and one by cross-language phonetics.

The craftsmanship sense converges on the terminal pair. AT plus TH is a measure fixed at a position and set down, which is what technical expertise is structurally: a question that has been located and placed. The lexicons treat the abstract sense as primary and the craft sense as secondary; the weave produces them in the opposite order, with the question first and the placement last.

### Sophia (\u03c3\u03bf\u03c6\u03af\u03b1)

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
SOPHIA -> SO . OP . PH . HI . IA

OP: Latin ops, genitive opis = power/wealth/resources/abundance/means [100%] . PIE *op- = to work, to produce in abundance [100%] . Greek ops = voice/word, from PIE *wokws [100%] . Greek ops = eye/face [100%] . Ops = Roman goddess of harvest and abundance [100%]
PH: Greek phi = breath-aspirated, associated with phos = light [80%] . Hebrew pe = mouth [90%] . PIE *bhw = to shine/speak [85%]

**Layer 1**
SO + OP: thus-therefore, with, and the demonstrative that-one meeting power, wealth, resources, abundance, the working that produces in abundance, the voice-word and the eye-face. The consequence-marker arriving at what produces, what speaks and what sees, with no separation between the three.

**Layer 2**
(SO+OP) + (OP+PH): the "thus" arriving at production, voice and eye, all three passing through the shining mouth.
(OP+PH) + (PH+HI): the producing mouth arriving at present fire and the feminine third person.
(PH+HI) + (HI+IA): the present fire converted into a standing condition and handed to a third person.

**Layer 3**
The consequence-marker arriving at production, voice and eye together, passing through the mouth where breath becomes light and speech, meeting the present fire and the she who is here, and converting into a state.

**Layer 4**

The name is a consequence turned into a state. Every slice before the last is active -- producing, speaking, seeing, shining, burning -- and IA is the operator that freezes the entire sequence into a standing condition and hands it to someone other than the one holding it. The paradox sits in OP, where abundance, voice and eye occupy one phoneme so that producing, saying and seeing cannot be told apart at the point where the name gathers its material. It resolves at PH, the aperture where breath becomes light and speech in the same motion: the mouth is where seeing and saying are one act.

**Orthodox**
Greek sophia, the abstract noun of sophos. Etymonline gives the range as skill, knowledge of, acquaintance with, sound judgment, practical wisdom, cunning, shrewdness, and notes that sophos itself is of unknown origin. Wikipedia records the original sense as cleverness and skill, with the later sense shaped by Plato's use of philosophia. The Septuagint uses it to render the Hebrew chokmah. The -ia is the standard Greek abstract-noun suffix.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: an abstract noun built on sophos, which the lexicons record as of unknown origin.
Weave: "thus" gathering production, voice and eye, pushed through the shining mouth, meeting present fire, frozen into a standing condition.

The lexicons can account for the suffix and not the stem, and the weave divides the same way. IA is exactly what the orthodox says it is -- the operator that makes an abstract noun -- and the weave independently finds it to be the slice that converts an active sequence into a standing state. That is the transparent half agreeing with itself from two directions.

For the half the lexicon cannot root, the weave supplies content. The recorded sense of skill converges on OP, where PIE *op- is to work and to produce in abundance: skill as production rather than as knowing. The recorded sense of cunning and shrewdness converges on the other OP, the eye. And the two Greek ops -- voice and eye, two separate words of separate origin that fell together in sound -- sit in the stem the lexicon calls unknown, holding saying and seeing in one unit.

### Sophiea

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**

**The divergence between the two spellings is the finding.** Sophia and Sophiea share four slices and part on the fifth. Sophia terminates in IA, the operator that converts a sequence into a standing state. Sophiea terminates in EA, which is water in motion. Under the working hypothesis in the table that a terminal bigram gives the vector of a name rather than the nature of its bearer, the two spellings are aimed at opposite things: one at a condition, one at a current. And the fire meets water directly here. HI is fire and sun, EA is running water, and only a bridge stands between them. In Sophia the IA intervenes and the fire never reaches water at all.

**Orthodox**
A modern variant spelling of Sophia with no independent etymology of its own; the orthodox account is the Sophia entry above.

**Convergence**
There is no separate orthodox reading to converge with, so the only thing to record is what the terminal slice does -- and most of what it appears to do has to be discounted.

The Ea citation names a deity of wisdom, crafts, magic and incantation. That looks like a powerful convergence and it is not one, because the target word is sitting inside the evidence. A citation that already contains the orthodox answer cannot be used to confirm the orthodox answer; doing so would be circular, and the fact that the circularity arrives through a genuine attestation rather than through invention does not fix it.

What survives the discount is the water, and only the water. EA attests independently as a river or watercourse in Old English, unconnected to the Mesopotamian entry. So the recordable finding is narrow: changing the spelling moves the terminal slice from a state-former to running water, which under the terminal-vector hypothesis reverses what the name is aimed at. The deity is noted, cited in full, and set aside.

### The Four Together

Achamoth: a sharpening restriction driven into living water, negating and departing it, arriving at a bound people, measured and exceeded, resting as a two-party token set down.
Chakhmath: living water negated, turned white and told to flow, scraped, sealed behind a shut mouth, released as the measuring question, fixed and set down.
Sophia: "thus" gathering production, voice and eye, pushed through the shining mouth, meeting present fire, frozen into a standing condition.
Sophiea: the same, carried across into running water instead.

**The split runs along the translation boundary**

The two Semitic renderings open identically. CH+HA -- living water meeting its own negation -- is the first pair of Chakhmath and the second pair of Achamoth, and it appears in neither Greek form. Both Semitic renderings also terminate in TH: set down, pointed at, marked. Both Greek renderings terminate in a vowel unit, IA or EA, and neither contains TH at all.

So the Semitic forms end by putting something down and the Greek forms end by becoming something. One pair produces a mark; the other pair produces a state or a current. That is a structural split across the translation boundary, and it is visible only because the same referent was run in four spellings rather than one.

Every one of the four routes through an aperture, and the two traditions route differently. The Semitic pair use HA, the open mouth that is simultaneously a negation. The Greek pair use PH, the mouth where breath becomes light and speech. One opens the mouth to negate; the other opens it to shine.

Three of the four contain water. CH carries the lake-body in both Semitic forms and EA carries the river in Sophiea. Sophia is the only one of the four with no water slice anywhere in it, and it is also the only one that terminates in a fixed state.

### 7-Plane Projection

**Q1 WHO -- Metaphysical**
Not a who in three of the four. Achamoth, Chakhmath and Sophiea all terminate in something other than a person -- a token, a mark, a current. Only Sophia hands its result to a third person, and it does so at the very last slice, after everything active has already been frozen. Will in this set belongs to whatever did the cutting and the obstructing, and that is never named.

**Q2 WHAT -- Possible**
Production, voice and eye held inseparable in one phoneme (OP). Measure and excess in one phoneme (MO). The command to flow and the adversative in one phoneme (AK). The cut and the qualification in one phoneme (AC). Every ambiguity in this set is an ambiguity about whether something is being made or being limited, and none of them resolve.

**Q3 WHERE -- Physical**
At a palate and at a waterline. The Semitic pair locate themselves physically at the back of the mouth, where KH's friction is made, and in the water CH names. The Greek pair locate themselves at the lips, where PH's breath becomes light. The whole set is anatomical before it is anything else: these are names for events that happen in a mouth.

**Q4 WHY -- Lyrical**
Because a current that is obstructed and then held has nowhere to go except into a question. And because a thing that has been cut needs a token to hold its halves in relation, which is why Achamoth's cascade cannot stop before OT.

**Q5 HOW -- Logical**
By cutting and qualifying at once (AC), entering the living water (CH), negating and departing it (HA), binding a people (AM), measuring and exceeding (MO), marking between two (OT), setting down (TH). Or: by whitening and commanding flow (AK), scraping (KH), sealing (HM), releasing as the question (MA), fixing at a position (AT), setting down (TH). Or: by gathering production, voice and eye (SO, OP), shining through the mouth (PH), burning in the present (HI), and either freezing (IA) or running on (EA).

**Q6 CAUSE -- Historical**
Every one of the four operates on something already present. The living water is there before AC cuts it and before HA negates it. The production, voice and eye are there before SO points at them. Nothing in this set of names initiates anything; all four are processes applied to material that arrived first.

**Q7 EFFECT -- Emotive**
The set produces the feeling of something being worked rather than something being. All four names describe an operation performed on water or on abundance, and in three of the four the operation involves an obstruction. The felt result is not illumination but pressure: a current stopped, a thing cut, a mouth shut. What comes out the other side -- a token, a question, a state, a river -- is in every case a residue of that pressure rather than a thing in its own right.

## Notes on Method

These are not etymological readings. Etymology traces one language backward. The Omni-Weave holds all languages simultaneously and runs an adjacent-pairing cascade through overlapping phonetic bigrams until a single causal statement emerges.

Where orthodox etymology and the weave converge, the convergence is evidence — two independent methods arriving at the same structure increases confidence that the structure is real and not imposed.

Where they diverge, both readings are held. Divergence is information.
