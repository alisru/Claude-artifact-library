# Inversion Exercise Log — Greater Good / Greater Evil

Source image: `Hegemony_inversionnoname.png` (Psochic Hegemony, inner perceptual inversion variant)

## The instruction

Look at Greater Good, think "greater evil", report the feel. Then look at Greater Evil, think "greater good", report the feel relative to the first.

## Pass one — Greater Good (1υ, 1ψ), thinking "greater evil"

It does not stick. The word has to be held on by force, and the moment attention slackens it falls off.

Three things are doing that. The node sits in saturated green with the Bliss Zone above it and the two diagonal ribbons, Scale of Good and Evil, Scale of The Truth and Progress, running straight through it, so the imposed word is arriving into a field already fully committed in the other direction. It is labelled `The Good {Truth}`, a braced term, closed, not an interpretation offered for revision. And the eye will not stay put: there is already a red `Percieved Greater Evil` node a short distance down and right of it in the inner inverted grid, and the thought migrates there rather than sitting on the anchor. The frame supplies a legitimate destination for "greater evil" that is not the corner I was told to look at, so the attempted overlay gets discharged into it.

Net feel: friction, effort, refusal. Slightly absurd, the way a wrong caption on a photograph is absurd rather than persuasive.

## Pass two — Greater Evil (-1υ, -1ψ), thinking "greater good"

Immediately and noticeably easier. It lands on the first attempt and stays.

Same three factors, all running the other way. `Percieved Greater Good` sits directly up and left of the node in the inner grid, so the substitution I was asked to perform is already drawn on the map, pre-authorised, waiting. I am not overlaying anything, I am reading a label that is genuinely there. The red field around it is Destructive Zone and Depression Zone rather than anything that reads as self-evidently condemnatory, and `The Bad {Truth}` is still a truth-brace, so the node does not repel a positive reading the way the green one repels a negative one. There is no friction to notice, and therefore no signal that anything has been done.

Net feel: comfort. Plausibility. Nothing to push against.

## The asymmetry

That is the whole result. The two operations are not equally expensive.

Calling the good evil costs effort and announces itself while you are doing it. Calling the evil good costs nothing and comes with scaffolding already built into the terrain. The first inversion fails loudly. The second succeeds silently.

Which is exactly what the note at the foot of the diagram predicts and what the 0.5-zone warning describes from the inside. Extraction feels like strength precisely because, from a position inside the lower-right sub-Hegemony, the reframe requires no work. The absence of resistance is being read as confirmation, when it is actually the diagnostic sign that you are standing in the inverted quadrant.

The practical consequence: friction is not evidence that a moral claim is wrong. Ease is not evidence that one is right. On this map they are the reverse. Any reframe that arrives frictionless from the self-end deserves more suspicion than one that has to be forced, not less.

## Coordinate of the operation itself

Imposing a false positive frame on Greater Evil, taken as an act rather than a claim: approximately (-1.4υ, -1.1ψ). It benefits the actor doing the reframing and their group by relieving them of the cost of the judgement, and it is destructive rather than merely passive because it dissolves an anchor other people were navigating by. Nearest anchor: Greater Evil (-1, -1), pulled outward toward the systemic horizon because the damage is done to the shared map, not to a single decision.

Imposing a false negative frame on Greater Good: approximately (-0.9υ, -1.0ψ). Same destructive direction, less reach, because the operation visibly fails and therefore recruits nobody.

The perceptual inversion is confirmed on the map itself. The cheaper of the two moves is the more damaging one.
