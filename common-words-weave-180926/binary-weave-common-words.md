# Binary Weave -- Common Words Test

Omni-Weave runs on common nouns rather than names, testing whether convergence behaviour differs between basic vocabulary (rain, fire) and constructed vocabulary (umbrella), and what the correct move is when a compound diverges from its source morpheme.

---

## Rain

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
RAIN → RA · AI · IN

RA: Hebrew ra (רַע) = evil/harm/bad [100%] · Egyptian Ra = sun/source [100%] · Sanskrit ra = to give/bestow [100%] · Arabic ra = to see/shepherd [100%] · Japanese ra (裸) = naked/bare/uncovered [100%] · Old Norse rá = corner/rafter [100%]
AI: Japanese ai (愛) = love [100%] · Greek ai = interjection of pain/grief [100%] · Hebrew ai (אַי) = where/woe [100%] · Sanskrit ai = to go toward [90%]
IN: English in = within [100%] · Latin in = in/not [100%] · German in = in [100%] · Sanskrit in = master/one-who-possesses [100%] · PIE *en gives in [100%]

**Layer 1**
RA + AI: the bare uncovered giver that is also the harm, going toward love and woe alike -- the seeing-shepherd arriving at the where-of-grief; the angled corner at the threshold of the lament
AI + IN: the going-toward that enters within and is possessed there -- the love-grief taken inside and mastered, or met by the not

**Layer 2**
(RA+AI) + (AI+IN): the bare source's bestowal, carrying harm and love together, going toward and entering to be possessed inside -- what the giver-that-wounds releases, arriving within as the held grief-love

**Layer 3**
The bare uncovered giver bestows what is harm and love at once, and it goes toward and is possessed within

**Layer 4**
The uncovered source releases what cannot be separated into gift or harm, and that release is only finished once it has gone toward something and been taken into possession inside it -- the giver does not retain it and the one who takes it in does not select it; what arrives is held within as both the loved and the wept-over, and the angled corner is where it collects.

**Orthodox**
Middle English rein, Old English regn, from Proto-Germanic *regna- (Old Saxon regan, Old Norse regn, Gothic rign), with no certain cognates outside Germanic -- unless (Watkins) from a presumed PIE *reg- = moist/wet, possible source of Latin rigare = to wet/moisten (behind irrigate), though de Vaan disputes the Germanic connection.

**Convergence**
The orthodox root is a Germanic isolate whose gloss is simply the referent, so there is no independent root meaning available to converge with. The one contested extension, *reg- = to moisten (rigare), operates on exactly the plane the cascade terminates in: IN, the within that possesses. Irrigation is the AI+IN movement made agricultural -- the going-toward that completes only by being taken inside. The RA split (giver and harm, held as a genuine non-converging dual in the table) is the referent's own dual, the same arrival being the crop and the flood. Convergence here is with the referent's mechanism rather than with a root gloss, because no root gloss exists.

## Fire

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
FIRE → FI · IR · RE

FI: French fi = interjection of disdain/rejection [100%] · Esperanto fi = fie/shameful [100%] · Jamaican Creole fi = for/to [100%] · Welsh fi = me/I [100%] · Arabic fī (في) = in/at/within [95% -- long vowel]
IR: Spanish ir = to go [100%] · Portuguese ir = to go [100%] · Latvian ir = is/there-is [100%] · Lithuanian ir = and/also [100%] · Welsh ir = fresh/green/verdant [100%] · Danish ir = verdigris/copper-patina [100%]
RE: Latin re- = again/back [100%] · Hebrew re (רֵעַ) = friend/neighbor/another [100%] · musical re = second note of the scale [100%] · Old French re- = again/anew [100%] · Greek rheo = to flow [90%]

**Layer 1**
FI + IR: what is held within and aimed at the self, cast out in rejection, meeting the going that simply is -- the shameful thing thrust forth arrives fresh and green, and also as the crust that forms on what has been exposed
IR + RE: the going-that-is meeting the again and the one beside -- motion and existence renewed, flowing back toward the neighbour; the fresh thing and the second one

**Layer 2**
(FI+IR) + (IR+RE): the self-held thing cast out as existence-in-motion, returning again toward another -- what was within goes forth, is, and repeats toward the one beside it

**Layer 3**
What is held within is cast out, goes forth as existence, and comes back again toward the other

**Layer 4**
What is contained is expelled as a motion that simply is, and that motion does not terminate but turns and arrives again at whatever stands beside it -- a structure that exists only while it goes, goes only by rejecting what held it, and continues only by reaching the next one over; the fresh green thing and the crust left on what it touched are the same process caught at two moments.

**Orthodox**
Old English fyr, from Proto-Germanic *fūr-, from PIE root *paewr- = fire. PIE had two roots for fire: *paewr-, the inanimate root naming fire as a substance, and *egni- (source of Latin ignis, Sanskrit agni), the animate root naming it as a living force.

**Convergence**
Stronger than expected once the cascade is allowed to finish on its own citations. The Hebrew re (neighbour, the one beside) turns the terminal into a movement toward what is adjacent, and adjacency-plus-repetition is the propagation behaviour of the referent, not a generic property of moving things. A river does not arrive at its neighbour and start again there. The Danish ir (the crust that forms on what has been exposed) is the residue left behind; the Welsh ir (fresh) is the leading edge. Those two citations sitting in the same bigram give both ends of the same event.

Divergent on substance: no bigram produces heat, light, or consuming, and that absence is held rather than repaired. The cascade reads the referent as a self-sustaining departure that must keep reaching the adjacent thing, which is a process description and not a thing description -- matching the fact that the English word descends from the inanimate substance-process root rather than the animate agent root. The weave reads the ontological category its own root belongs to.

## Umbrella

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
UMBRELLA → UM · MB · BR · RE · EL · LL · LA

UM: German um = around/about [100%] · Old Norse/Icelandic um = around/concerning [100%] · Portuguese um = one/a [100%] · Serbian/Croatian/Czech/Russian um (ум) = mind/intellect/skill [100%] · English um = hesitation-pause [100%]
MB: structural -- nasal-into-stop cluster, the hum closed into a hard stop; no standalone cross-language citation found
BR: onomatopoetic burr/vibration -- the rolled-R trembling as a standalone unit; the brr shiver as its felt consequence; no other standalone attestation
RE: Latin re- = again/back [100%] · Hebrew re (רֵעַ) = friend/neighbor/another [100%] · musical re = second note [100%] · Old French re- = again/anew [100%] · Greek rheo = to flow [90%]
EL: Hebrew El (אֵל) = God/the-strong-one [100%] · Phoenician/Aramaic ʾl = god [100%] · PIE *el = to flow/move [100%]
LL: structural -- intensifying doubling of the lateral; the flowing sound extended to completion
LA: French la = the (feminine definite) [100%] · Spanish la = the/her [100%] · Italian la = the/her [100%] · Arabic lā (لا) = no/not [95% -- long vowel] · Portuguese lá = there [100%] · Chinese lā (拉) = to pull/draw [100%] · Norwegian la = to let [100%] · solfège la = sixth note [100%]

**Layer 1**
UM + MB: the around-one and the mind that holds it, brought up short at the hard stop -- encirclement and unity halted, the pause sealed
MB + BR: the halt trembling -- the sealed stop set buzzing, the closure that shivers
BR + RE: the tremble again and again -- the shiver renewed, flowing back toward the one beside
RE + EL: the again meeting the strong-one and the flowing -- the return arriving at force in motion
EL + LL: the strong flow doubled and extended to completion -- the current held to its full extent
LL + LA: the completed extension meeting the pointed-at definite that refuses, that is there, that pulls and that lets

**Layer 2**
(UM+MB) + (MB+BR): the encircling one held trembling at the stop
(MB+BR) + (BR+RE): the trembling halt repeating -- the shiver returning against the sealed closure
(BR+RE) + (RE+EL): the renewed tremble at the strong flow -- vibration sustained by the current
(RE+EL) + (EL+LL): the returning strong flow extended to its full completion
(EL+LL) + (LL+LA): the fully extended current arriving at the definite refusal -- the no that is there, that draws and that lets

**Layer 3**
The around-one, halted and trembling, taking the strong flow repeated and extended to completion, and meeting it with the no that pulls and lets

**Layer 4**
The encircling one is brought to a stop and held trembling there while a strong current arrives again and again and builds to its full extent, and the structure completes at its far end in the refusal -- the no that is there, that draws what arrives toward itself and then lets it go. Its whole function is to be the one-around, halted, that receives repeatedly and keeps nothing.

**Orthodox**
Late Latin umbrella, altered (by influence of umbra) from Latin umbella = sunshade/parasol, diminutive of umbra = shade/shadow. Entered English in the early 17th century via Italian ombrella. The Latin names a sun-device; the Greeks introduced Europeans to the sunshade and the Romans extended it to rain.

**Convergence**
Divergent on the root: nothing in the cascade yields shade, shadow, or darkness. The sun-metaphor the Latin encodes is entirely absent.

Convergent on the object as it now functions: the around-one held rigid at the stop, trembling under a repeated current (EL = flow, doubled and extended by LL), refusing it at the far end (Arabic lā = no) and letting it run off (Norwegian la = to let). The weave reads the working device; the etymology preserves a fossil metaphor the object has outgrown. The terminal-vector hypothesis holds -- the final bigram aims the whole structure at refusal-and-release, which is the edge where the device does its work.

## Umbra -- Source-Morpheme Run

Where a compound diverges from its orthodox root, the combinationary move is to descend one morphological level and weave the source morpheme: the compound's phonetics carry the assembled object, the morpheme's phonetics carry the original concept.

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
UMBRA → UM · MB · BR · RA

UM: German um = around/about [100%] · Old Norse/Icelandic um = around/concerning [100%] · Portuguese um = one/a [100%] · Serbian/Croatian/Czech/Russian um (ум) = mind/intellect/skill [100%] · English um = hesitation-pause [100%]
MB: structural -- nasal-into-stop cluster, the hum halted; no standalone citation
BR: onomatopoetic burr/vibration; the brr shiver
RA: Hebrew ra = evil/harm/bad [100%] · Egyptian Ra = sun/source [100%] · Sanskrit ra = to give/bestow [100%] · Arabic ra = to see/shepherd [100%] · Japanese ra = naked/bare/uncovered [100%] · Old Norse rá = corner/rafter [100%]

**Layer 1**
UM + MB: the around-one and the mind that holds it, halted at the hard stop -- encirclement brought up short
MB + BR: the halt trembling -- the sealed stop shivering
BR + RA: the shiver at the bare uncovered giver that also wounds -- the cold held at the seeing-source, at the angled corner

**Layer 2**
(UM+MB) + (MB+BR): the encircling one held trembling at the stop
(MB+BR) + (BR+RA): the trembling halt at the bare source that gives and harms

**Layer 3**
The around-one, halted and trembling, at the bare uncovered giver that wounds

**Layer 4**
The one-around comes to a stop and stays there shivering at the bare giver that also harms -- its entire condition is the halt it makes at the uncovered source; an encirclement whose only content is the cold it holds while turned toward what gives, gathered at the angled corner.

**Orthodox**
Latin umbra = shade/shadow. In English from the 1590s as phantom/ghost, the astronomical shadow-cone sense recorded by the 1670s.

**Convergence -- Partial**
The terminal bigram of the Latin word for shadow is RA, the source itself, and under the terminal-vector hypothesis the final bigram is where the force is aimed. The cascade produces a halt aimed at the giver, which is the harder half of the orthodox meaning, since the referent is a thing defined wholly by its relation to a light source and by nothing else. The BR shiver sits inside the word for the one place that stays cold. The Old Norse rá (corner/rafter, the angled edge) matches the geometry of the thing, which is a cone.

What the cascade does not produce is the absence. Nothing in UM·MB·BR·RA yields negation, blocking, or darkness. The weave gives the relation to the source and the coldness; the orthodox supplies the failure of light to arrive. Enough to show the descent move works -- compound-level divergence sends the run down one morphological level and convergence resumes -- but a partial hit, not a strong one.

## Findings -- Common Words vs Names

Three distinct convergence targets emerged from one method.

**Basic words with isolate or self-referential etymologies (rain).** The orthodox root glosses as the referent itself, so there is no independent root meaning to converge with, and convergence has to be measured against the referent's mechanism instead. Rain hit on mechanism: the disputed *reg- = to-moisten line operates on the IN plane the cascade terminates in, and the RA giver-harm dual is the referent's own crop-and-flood dual.

**Basic words where the cascade produces a process rather than a substance (fire).** No heat, light or consuming appears anywhere in the citations, and that absence is held. What the cascade does produce -- a motion that must keep arriving at the adjacent thing to continue -- is propagation, which is specific to the referent rather than generic to moving things, and it matches the inanimate substance-process root the English word actually descends from rather than the animate agent root PIE kept alongside it. The weave lands in the correct half of a split the language itself made.

**Constructed and compound words (umbrella).** The compound's cascade reads the assembled object's current function; the etymology preserves a fossil metaphor the object has outgrown. Where compound-level divergence appears, descend one morphological level and weave the source morpheme. Umbra converged partially -- terminal RA gives the relation to the source, the absence half is missing -- enough to support the descent move as the combinationary method for compounds without overstating what it recovered.

Working generalisation to test on further runs: names converge with orthodox etymology, common nouns converge with referent mechanism, compounds converge only after descent to the source morpheme.
