## 1. The thesis

Anything new is subject to BCS, the best in the span of causal time.

What is new can only be made from what already is. This is not an observation about scarcity or difficulty. It is a claim about what making is. To make is to take what is and put it into a state it was not in. There is no other operation available to anything that is itself inside time.

The output of a making is the exact resultant of the conditions present at that moment. Not roughly what was there, exactly what was there. Perfect reason, perfect cause and effect, operating on precisely those conditions. Nothing missing, nothing wasted, nothing that could have gone another way given what was present.

"Best" here is not a grade against an ideal. There is no shortfall to grade, because there is no co-available alternative the output fell short of. An ideal made of material that does not yet exist is not an alternative. It is a description of a different world with a different history.

You can only use what is from what was to make what will be.

The universe is included, because it was new once, and causality applies to it too. Section 8 treats that case, and it is the case the rest of the paper is built to reach.

## 2. The frame

### 2.1 A moment is the state

A moment is not a container that a state sits inside. There is no empty t waiting to be occupied. The moment is the state, and the state is what its components hold.

The unit is the Totality Event Frame:

Totality_Event_Frame[when] = [ past[when_prev], present[when_now], future[when_nextPredicted] ]

where

when = [ who[when], what[when], why[when], where[when], how[when], cause[when], effect[when] ]

The consequence of writing it this way is that *when* is not a coordinate the seven components are indexed to. *When* is the seven components. Time is not a line the states are arranged along. It is the repeated closure of tuples, each closed one becoming the material of the next.

### 2.2 The seven components

**Who. The metaphysical plane. Will and direction.** What the actor is aiming at.

**What. The possible plane. Faith and probability.** Whether the thing has been individuated at all, and with what confidence. Nothing can be aimed at, located, explained or traced until it has been picked out as a something.

**Where. The physical plane. Matter and distance.** What is materially at hand, and how far away the rest of it is.

**Why. The lyrical plane. Meaning and resonance.** Whether a reason for the act is available and legible in that setting. This is a real constraint on whether an act can be performed, not merely on whether it can be conceived. An act with no available meaning in its setting is not a live option in that setting.

**How. The logical plane. Count and consistency.** Which operations can actually be performed. Operations are made things, and section 5 treats them.

**Cause. The historical plane. Sequence and causality.** How much of the chain producing the present state can be traced.

**Effect. The emotive plane. Passion and consequence.** What can be foreseen as following, and what the setting will do in response.

### 2.3 Six bound, one drives

Six of the seven bound what can be produced. Who does not.

In the 42-structure, Who is the emergent axis, the seventh angle, the driver. The remaining six form three paired axes.

The lateral axis, body, definition and space: possible against physical.

The longitudinal axis, mind, function and meaning: lyrical against logical.

The vertical axis, soul, the temporal link: historical against emotive.

Who is not a member of any of the three. That is not an omission. A frame is the space. Will is the direction of travel through it. Making will a component of the space would make the actor a feature of their own option set, which removes the distinction between the space and the movement through it.

This also explains why the fill order in section 3 begins at What rather than at Who. The order is the order in which a frame closes, and Who is not a thing that gets filled. It is what does the filling.

### 2.4 The three slots

Past, present and future are not three kinds of thing. They are one register in three completion states.

Past holds complete tuples. Future holds the tuple currently being filled toward. Present is neither.

When the future slot is complete, it becomes a past slot, and the cycle runs again.

The asymmetry is completion, not certainty and not kind. This matters, because reading it as a difference in kind, actuals on one side and guesses on the other, produces a different and wrong account of why BCS runs backward. It runs backward because it applies only to closed tuples, and closure is what makes a tuple past.

### 2.5 Present is the operation, not a slot

Past and future hold tuples. Present is where filling happens.

So the present is the only position at which a tuple is incomplete and action occurs anyway. Everything done is done from there, on a state that is by definition not finished. Section 9 draws the consequence.

### 2.6 The frame holds a model of its own successor

The future slot is filled from the present frame. It is produced there, out of what the present frame contains, and it is therefore part of the present frame rather than something outside it.

Which means the frame holds a model of what comes next, and that model is among the conditions constraining what the successor actually becomes.

Anticipation therefore constrains action with no backward causation anywhere. What does the work is a present-tense model, held now, made from what is here now. Nothing reaches back from a future state, because there is no future state yet to reach.

### 2.7 Effect is not circular

Read flat, an Effect component inside a present tuple looks circular: a state containing its own consequences.

With three slots it is not. Past holds actualised effect. Present holds registering effect. Future holds modelled effect. One plane, three completion states, no self-containment.

## 3. Fill order

### 3.1 The order

A frame does not close by having its components filled in any sequence.

What comes first. Then where, why, how, cause and effect. Complete information cannot be got otherwise.

### 3.2 Why What is prior

The other six are components *of* something.

A thing not yet picked out as a thing has no location to fill, because there is nothing for the location to be the location of. It has no available meaning, no mechanism, no traceable cause and no consequence, for the same reason in each case. There is no referent for the remaining questions to be about.

This is not a claim about the order in which data can be gathered. Data can plainly be gathered ahead of What. Positional measurements, correlations, effects and procedures can all be accumulated before anyone has fixed what they are measurements or effects of.

The claim is that gathered ahead of What, they do not bind. They are attributed to the wrong thing, rejected for want of something to attach to, or come back empty. The frame stays open.

### 3.3 What filled wrongly: Le Verrier twice

Uranus deviated from its predicted path. Le Verrier filled What as an unseen perturbing mass, computed Where from that filling, and sent coordinates to Berlin. Galle found Neptune on 23 September 1846, within one degree of the predicted position.

The same man then applied the same method to Mercury's perihelion discrepancy, which he had himself identified in 1859 from timed transit observations reaching back to 1697. He filled What as a planet interior to Mercury and named it Vulcan. Where was computed from that filling. Nothing was there. Nothing was ever found there.

The procedure was identical in both cases. The difference in outcome is entirely a difference in the first component. Where is downstream of What, and where What is wrong, Where does not merely come out wrong. It comes out empty, and the frame never closes.

Mercury's frame closed in 1915, when the discrepancy was filled with a What that was not an object at all.

### 3.4 How left empty: Wegener

Wegener filled What and Where for continental drift in 1912. The fit of the coastlines, the fossil distributions across separated landmasses, and the matching geological structures were all in hand, and they were not disputed as observations.

How was empty. The driving forces he proposed, polflucht and tidal friction, were calculated as orders of magnitude too weak to move continents through ocean floor.

The frame stayed open for fifty years. It closed when Hess published seafloor spreading in 1962, which supplied a mechanism in which continents do not plough through the ocean floor but are carried as it spreads.

The observations did not improve in the interval. The component that was missing was filled.

### 3.5 What left unstable: Semmelweis

Semmelweis filled Effect and How at the Vienna General Hospital in 1847. He ordered examiners to scrub in chlorinated lime, and the maternal mortality series fell sharply and stayed down.

What remained "cadaverous particles". The Effect was measured, the How was specified and repeatable, and the frame did not close, because the thing being acted on had not been individuated.

It closed when germ theory supplied the What.

### 3.6 One mechanism

These are not three categories of failure. They are one: components filled out of order do not bind, so the frame does not close, so it never passes into the state where BCS applies to it.

## 4. Micro-span

### 4.1 A frame is indexed to an actor, not to a date

A frame indexed to a time alone is a period, and periods are not option sets.

"The span of 1838" bundles a minister drafting instructions in a capital with a stockman on a frontier run and treats them as facing one set of options. They shared no material, no operations, no information and no incentives. Anything asserted about what "was available" in that year is true of one of them and false of the other, or true of neither.

People are brought up differently. Some optimise for self-benefit and some for other-benefit, usually self-benefit in self-benefit environments. Conditioning, information and sub-culture vary within any population, which is why factions exist inside every one of them, and why nothing follows from a claim about what "the era" could have done.

So the coordinates have to be given before any claim about a frame can be assessed: whose frame, at what position, open in which respects.

### 4.2 What the index buys

Without the index, the same evidence supports two incompatible readings and nothing distinguishes them. Either every outcome is a failure to take an available option, or every apparent failure is somebody's absent option. Both are unfalsifiable.

With the index, the coordinates have to be stated, and then the question has a determinate answer.

### 4.3 A frame has a shape

A set has only a size. Frames are not equally open in all respects, and two frames of the same size can be closed in entirely different places. That is the difference between a moment that is rich and produces nothing and a moment that is thin and produces a great deal.

The three paired axes give six characteristic ways a frame can be lopsided.

**Wide in matter, narrow in individuation.** Holding everything needed and not knowing what one is holding. Semmelweis.

**Wide in individuation, narrow in matter.** Knowing exactly what is wanted and having none of it.

**Wide in meaning, narrow in mechanism.** A pattern that is legible with no account of how it works. Wegener.

**Wide in mechanism, narrow in meaning.** A procedure that works with no available account of why it should, which in most settings is not adopted.

**Wide in cause, narrow in effect.** A long view backward and none forward.

**Wide in effect, narrow in cause.** Consequences foreseen with no grasp of what produced the present state.

## 5. What is in a frame

A frame is not a stock of material. Four kinds of thing are in it, and all four are made.

### 5.1 Material

The physical stock at hand. This is the least contentious component and needs no argument.

### 5.2 Operations

An operation is a made thing.

To get to 1 you need to accumulate from 0, and invent 0.1, and addition. Zero had to be invented. So did addition. Neither is a free-standing capacity that was always available to be picked up.

An actor holding all the material for a thing while lacking the operation that combines it is bounded as firmly as one holding no material at all. This is a distinct closure. Material closure alone does not describe it, and an account that only tracks stock will read such a frame as open when it is shut.

### 5.3 Individuations

Nothing can be aimed at until it has been picked out as a something. Section 3 treats the consequence.

### 5.4 Information

Even the information held is causally limited.

Knowledge is produced from what was already present, by operations that were themselves produced. No actor can step outside its information state at the moment of acting in order to act on a better one, because acquiring information is an act, made from what is there.

All people use the information available to them, per their conditioning and reasoning.

This closes the gap that material closure alone leaves open. Without it, the constraint on material can be granted and the constraint on knowledge denied, treating knowledge as a free variable that could have been set higher at no causal cost. It could not. The information state at t is the output of the frame at t minus one, exactly as the material is.

## 6. Formal statement

### 6.1 Axioms

**A1. Material closure.** Every made thing is made from what already exists. Nothing enters a making from outside the frame obtaining at the time of the making.

**A2. Causal sufficiency.** Every event is fully accounted for by what preceded it. There is no residue in an effect that is not traceable to its causes.

**A3. Finitude.** Every made thing occupies, consumes or excludes some portion of what exists. Nothing finite comes to be without something else being unable to be, in that place, of that material, at that time.

**A4. Frame closure.** The operations, individuations and information held at a moment are themselves made things, and are subject to A1.

### 6.2 Definitions

**D1.** F(a, t) is the frame of actor a at t: the seven components in their filled or unfilled state, in the sense of section 2.

**D2.** S(a, t) is the set of things producible from F(a, t).

**D3.** A making is a transition from F(a, t) to some x present at a later t, where x was not present.

**D4. BCS.** For any made x arising from F(a, t): x is a member of S(a, t), and x is the resultant of the conditions in F(a, t).

### 6.3 Theorem 1. Non-comparativity

*Claim.* There is no y such that x fell short of y.

*Proof.* "x fell short of y" requires three things at once: that y was producible from F(a, t); that the conditions in F(a, t) could have yielded y; and that they yielded x instead.

Two cases.

For any y outside S(a, t), the first condition fails directly. By A1, nothing outside the frame is producible from it at all.

For any y inside S(a, t), the first two conditions may hold, and the third requires that y was better and was passed over anyway. But by A4 the information state is itself the output of the prior frame, and selection among producible options proceeds on that state and no other. An actor who produced x rather than y did so because x was the better option on the information at hand. Declining an option is itself a selection performed on that information, not an exemption from it. So there is no member of the span that was producible, better on the state the actor actually held, and passed over regardless. ∎

*Remark.* One can argue that a person should have done infinite other things in infinite other ways from one point in time to the next. They did not, because they were acting on and acted upon by internal and external information at that moment.

*Remark on determinism.* The proof does not require that the frame determined a single output. Under a strong reading of A2 the span holds one member and there is nothing to select among. Under a weak reading it holds several, and A4 fixes which is selected. The conclusion is the same on either reading, so the result does not stand or fall with determinism.

### 6.4 Theorem 2. Iteration is necessary

*Claim.* If x requires m, and m is not in F(a, t), then x is not producible at t, and no quantity of power, knowledge or intention changes it.

*Proof.* By A1, x is made from what is in the frame. If m is absent, m is not available to the making, and if x cannot be made without m, x is not in S(a, t). Since m is itself a made thing, m has its own requirement m', and so on. Producing x therefore requires an ordered sequence of makings, each bringing into being the material of the next. ∎

*Corollary.* Iteration is not a method chosen over a faster alternative. It is the mechanism by which the material of any end state comes to exist. There is no version of the end that arrives before the sequence, because the end is made of the sequence's outputs.

This is what answers "why was it not just made right". That question asks for a finished state without the chain that produces its material. It is not asking why the slow way was taken. It is asking why an end can exist before its parts.

### 6.5 Theorem 3. The perfection request

*Claim.* "State P should have obtained at t0" requests the suspension of A1 or A4.

*Proof.* Either P is in S(a, t0) or it is not.

If it is not, then by A1 P was not producible from F(a, t0), and the request is that something be made out of what is not there.

If it is, and P was not produced, then by Theorem 1 it was not what looked better on the information held, and the request is that the actor have selected on some information state other than the one its own history produced. That is a suspension of A4. ∎

*Remark.* Both are the same request in different clothing. One asks for absent material, the other for absent knowledge. In neither case is there a second state of affairs for the comparison to refer to.

*What suspending A1 costs.* If effects need not follow from their material, no state is made of any prior state. If no state is made of any prior state, there is no sequence in which one thing leads to another. Growth, choice, consequence and time as succession all require later states to be constituted out of earlier ones. What remains after the suspension is not a kinder world running on the same rules. It is a still image.

### 6.6 Theorem 4. Completion

*Claim.* BCS applies to closed tuples only.

*Proof.* The claim BCS makes is about the exactness of an output relative to the conditions that produced it. Until the last component is filled, the tuple is still filling, and there is no output yet for the claim to be about. ∎

*Three positions follow.*

A closed tuple admits the claim in full. What issued from it was the exact output of it, and the event was necessary in the only sense causality supports.

A filling tuple admits no claim at all, since part of what will close it is what the actor is about to do. An actor reasoning "whatever I do will have been the exact output" has mistaken an open tuple for a closed one, which is not reading the frame but declining to be one of the things that closes it.

A tuple not yet begun is not a tuple. It is the space the current one will produce.

*Consequence.* BCS cannot function as an excuse. An excuse is offered from the present, about an act being performed or about to be. BCS does not reach the present. It is not that using it as an excuse is prohibited; the operation does not run there. By the time the claim holds, the tuple has closed and there is nothing left to excuse.

All events in the past are by evidence BCS. The conditions of the previous frames set up the frames that followed, precisely. This declares events one hundred per cent necessary to have occurred the ways they did, after they happened.

## 7. Material precedence

The universe is a multidimensional resource: a collection of whats, wheres, whys, hows, causes and effects. Each frame draws from the one before it. Four cases.

### 7.1 Bronze and iron

Bronze was not a failed attempt at iron.

The temperatures are not close. Iron work requires sustained conditions far above what bronze work reaches, on the order of 1480 degrees against roughly 920. The bloomery that eventually produced usable iron depends on prepared charcoal, close to pure carbon, supplying both the heat and the carbon monoxide that reduces the ore; on tuyeres admitting air; and on bellows or draught to force it.

The practice that built furnaces, worked out draught and made charcoal reliably at scale was bronze-working practice. Iron was not something bronze fell short of, because iron was not there to fall short of. Bronze was the exact output of its frame, and part of that output was the material of iron's frame.

The pattern repeats one level up. The higher temperatures that blast furnaces require came from successive improvements in heating, and furnaces dedicated to molten iron were not built until the fourteenth century.

### 7.2 Newton and Einstein

Newtonian mechanics was the exact output of what could be observed in 1687.

General relativity required three things that did not exist then. Mercury's perihelion discrepancy was identified by Le Verrier in 1859 from timed transit observations reaching back to 1697, which required a century and a half of accumulated positional astronomy and could not have been done sooner because the observations did not exist. The Michelson-Morley null result came in 1887 and required an interferometer sensitive enough to detect nothing. Maxwell's equations were in place by then.

Relativity was published in 1915 into a frame that held the anomaly, the null result and the field equations together. The earlier state is not a worse version of the later one. It is what the later one is made of.

### 7.3 Carnivory

Carnivores did not choose meat in any deliberate sense.

Cats cannot synthesise taurine in sufficient quantity and must take it from diet. Feeding cysteinesulfinic acid as a putative precursor produces no significant increase in taurine in plasma, whole blood or muscle. They cannot make arginine. They cannot convert beta-carotene to vitamin A and so require retinol from meat.

Dollo's law names the general case. Once the developmental pathway or genetic structure underlying a complex trait is lost, the trait does not reappear in its original form.

Choice here means accumulated causal commitment. A lineage specialised around available fuel, and once specialised the route back closed. That is causality running over generations, and the resulting animal is the exact output of the frames its lineage occupied.

### 7.4 Lock-in

The decisions available now are limited by decisions already made, even where the original conditions no longer obtain. Path dependence is the same structure described by economists rather than biologists. The present is never a clean slate, because it is made of what preceded it.

## 8. The limit case: making from nothing

### 8.1 Why this is the limit case and not an instance

Every complaint about the world reduces to a complaint about one frame.

The universe was defined from formless infinity in the first few moments of time itself. Everything after is made from what that resolution produced, and everything after that from what the next one produced. The chain has one end, and this is it.

So creation is not one application of BCS among others. It is the only frame with no predecessor. Every other frame in this paper, every furnace and every observation and every lineage, is downstream of it, and the whole of section 7 is a set of consequences of what that frame produced.

It is also where every version of the perfection request terminates. "Why was the eleventh century not better" resolves into "why was the world not made better", which resolves into a complaint about the resolution of formlessness in the first few moments. The small cases are worth stating first because the same move is far harder to see at the largest scale.

### 8.2 Light

Take the origin frame epistemically.

God is a being of information, prior to the physical universe. At that frame, information is what is there. By A1, whatever is made first must be made from what is there. So the first thing made is light, because information is the only available material.

Light is information.

A sun is a source of information other than God.

That is the whole of the ordering. First information. Then sources of information other than the origin. A source of a thing cannot precede the thing it is a source of, which is Theorem 2 in its first instance and its cleanest one, because at that frame there is nothing else in play to complicate it.

Nothing in this requires light at the origin to be electromagnetic radiation, and nothing in it is a correction applied later to fix an ordering. The ordering is the only one A1 permits given what the frame contains.

### 8.3 Seven moments

The resolution took the span it took. Formless infinity to a structured universe in something on the order of the first few moments of time.

Under Theorem 2 that span is not a duration chosen over a shorter one. Each moment produced the material of the next, and the sequence could not be compressed, because compressing it would require an end state to precede the things it is made of.

The number of moments is therefore a fact about how many stages the material required, not about how long the maker took.

### 8.4 The uncaused term

By A1 and A3, every made thing takes from what already is. So the sequence either runs back without end, or it terminates in something that makes without taking.

Only something uncaused, depending on nothing before itself, could make without taking. Everything else has to take, because everything else is made of what came before it.

This follows from the axioms rather than being assumed by them. The slot is required. What fills it is a separate question, and the results above hold whichever way that question is answered.

### 8.5 Taking is the price of being finite

Taking is not a flaw that slipped into the design. It is what being a finite thing that lasts through time means.

Eating a plant ends a living process that was turning sunlight into more of itself, and that plant was already drawing on soil, water and light that something else could have used. Occupying ground is itself a form of taking, since ground used for one thing cannot be used for another. Even photosynthesis draws down a supply the plant did not make.

There is no way to eat inside a finite body that is not taking from something, and no way to be in a place without something else not being there.

A universe where nothing took from anything would be a universe with no finite things in it. Taking is the price of anything besides the uncaused term existing as a separate thing in time at all.

### 8.6 Taking is not one quantity

That taking is unavoidable does not make all taking equivalent.

Two properties of what is taken decide where a particular taking sits.

**Renewability.** Whether what is taken grows back in the time that matters.

**Rarity.** How scarce it is relative to what the surroundings need to remain viable.

Taking from something abundant and fast-regrowing and taking from something scarce and slow-regrowing are different acts, though both are literally taking. The first leaves the next frame roughly intact. The second narrows it.

Mapped onto the two-axis measure, where υ tracks who an act benefits and ψ tracks what the energy is doing, taking does not sit at the destructive end of ψ merely because it is unavoidable. Where it sits depends on whether the take outruns what can regrow, given how scarce the thing actually is there. Viability decides the placement, not the bare fact of taking.

### 8.7 The universe as locus

To all effect the universe acts like a wand. A locus. A focus where everyone's infinite potential and God's can be gathered and expressed in any form of order at all.

Man does the same thing through simulations, making universes inside the universe with no rules or laws except self-imposed ones. That is man copying the act that made him, not arriving at it independently.

And man does not build a simulation on a whim. He builds it for reasons, and they are the same reasons the universe was made, because the reason is part of what is being copied.

## 9. Suffering

### 9.1 The definition

Suffering is acting with a lack of understanding.

It sits in the act, not in a sensation. Which means it is never located in whoever it lands on. Pain lands. Suffering is done.

### 9.2 It is a property of sequencing

*Claim.* Suffering is not an occasional event within the sequence. It is a property of sequencing itself.

*Proof.* By A4, the information in any frame is the output of the frame before it, and is therefore partial: it holds what has been produced and nothing else. No actor holds the information that later frames will produce, because that information does not yet exist to be held. By section 2.5, the present is the only position at which action occurs, and it is by definition the position where the tuple is unfinished. Every act therefore issues from a partial state. By the definition above, an act performed with a lack of understanding is suffering. So suffering is produced continuously rather than intermittently. ∎

This supplies the mechanism the definition alone does not. It says why suffering is produced at every frame rather than at some, and the answer is not malice, scarcity or misfortune. It is that acting from a partial state is the only kind of acting available inside time.

### 9.3 It is symmetrical

Two parties can each be aiming at what they take to be good, each acting on the best information available to them, and harm is produced by both, with nothing overriding anywhere in the chain.

That is the ordinary case, not the tragic exception. Where it looks as though one party is aiming at good and the other at bad, that reading is generally an artefact of a partial information state, held by whoever is doing the reading.

### 9.4 A universe with no perception of suffering

God has made a universe in which there is no perception of suffering. It is this one.

That reads as false only if suffering is taken to be something perceived. It is not. It is in the act, so it is never located in whoever it lands on.

### 9.5 The tool question

The better question is whether a tool can be made that cannot be used to create suffering in others. Since the universe is that locus, the question is about the universe.

The answer follows from the definition. The tool never creates suffering, because the tool has no understanding to lack. Only the one holding it does. There is nothing that could be built into the wand to fix this, because the thing that produces suffering was never in the wand.

## 10. What BCS is for

BCS is not a moral judgement and is not meant to have an effect. It is a statement on the nature of causality.

Its use is to put to rest claims of the form "the eleventh century should have had nukes."

That claim, and everything shaped like it, requires an alternative. Specifying the alternative means specifying material, operations, individuations or information that were not there, which specifies a different frame, which specifies a different world. The comparison is then between two unrelated things, one of them invented.

Alternative history is good for storymaking. As argument it retards discourse, and it terminates in the same place every time, which is a critique of the being who made the universe from formless infinity in the span of a few moments.

BCS does not sort history into piles. It does not weigh what someone could have done otherwise, because there is no otherwise to weigh. The conditions that produced the act are exactly the conditions that were there.

## 11. Mistakes and regret

Mistakes can be made. Regret is impossible.

A mistake is a property of one closed frame, readable off that frame alone. Vulcan is a mistake: What was filled wrongly, so Where never filled. Establishing that requires no second frame and no alternative history. It requires the frame and its contents.

Regret is not a property. It is a relation. It sets a closed frame against a second closed frame that would have issued from the same conditions, and that second frame is exactly what does not exist. The frame produced the outcome exactly and without remainder, so any alternative requires different conditions, and different conditions are a different frame.

Regret has the grammar of a comparison and no second term.

The structure makes it stricter. Regret is an act performed from the present, the only position where acting occurs, and directed at a closed tuple, the only position that takes no further filling. It is work done in the one place work happens, aimed at the one place it cannot land. And by section 9 it is an act from a partial state, which makes it a producer of the thing it is reacting to.

What survives is correction. Correction operates on the open tuple and takes the closed one as input, which is A1 restated: what was made becomes what the next thing is made from. The mistake stops being a debt and becomes material.

Regret treats a closed frame as though it were still fillable. Correction treats it as stock.
