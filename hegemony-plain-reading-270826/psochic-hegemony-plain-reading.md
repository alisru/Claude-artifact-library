# The Psochic Hegemony: A Plain Reading of the Map

*A ground-level explanation of the compass, its parts, its named points, its decorative layers, and where its vocabulary comes from.*

---

## 1. The whole thing in two questions

The map asks two questions and nothing else. Everything printed on it is an answer to one of them or a consequence of both.

**Question one: who does this benefit?**
That is the horizontal axis, υ. One end is everyone, including people the actor will never meet. The other end is the actor alone. The exact middle is no one.

**Question two: what is the will doing?**
That is the vertical axis, ψ. The top is acting, building, producing, making the thing happen. The bottom is withholding, allowing, suppressing, letting the thing rot.

Cross the two and any action, policy, statement, institution or person becomes a point. The point is the judgement. Nothing else is required to use the instrument.

The scoring range on each axis runs +2 to -2. The bare mechanics:

**υ (who benefits)**
- +2 everyone, all beings, systemic justice
- +1 other people, the greater good
- 0 no one
- -1 my group only
- -2 only me, pure extraction

**ψ (what the will does)**
- +2 actively creating systemic value for all
- +1 proactive, creating, building, acting
- 0 no meaningful force applied
- -1 passive, allowing, suppressing, withholding
- -2 actively destroying or extracting

Report the result as a decimal pair, name the nearest anchor, state the verdict in plain language. Do not moralise. Map only.

---

## 2. The four corners

At the ±1 ring, four named anchors sit at the diagonals. These are the load-bearing parts of the diagram and the fastest way to teach it.

**Greater Good (+1υ, +1ψ). The Good Truth.**
Building something that helps everyone. Intent and action point the same way and both point outward.

**Lesser Evil (-1υ, +1ψ). The Bad Lie.**
Building something that helps only the actor. It is *lesser* because production is still happening. Energy is going into the world, it is simply aimed inward. The lie is in the mismatch: outward-facing motion, inward-facing benefit.

**Lesser Good (+1υ, -1ψ). The Good Lie.**
Withholding, but the withholding helps others. Holding your tongue. Declining to act because acting would cost someone else. The claim is indifference; the effect is shelter.

**Greater Evil (-1υ, -1ψ). The Bad Truth.**
Withholding what would have helped, and the withholding serves only the actor. Nothing is produced, nobody is fed, and there is no pretence otherwise.

### The two diagonals

This is the part that turns the map from a scoring rubric into a lie detector.

The Greater Good and the Greater Evil sit on the same diagonal because **both are honest**. One says "I am helping and I am helping." The other says "I am not helping, and I am not pretending to." The stated and the actual agree in both cases. This is the truth diagonal, marked on the illustration as the Scale of Good and Evil.

The Lesser Evil and the Lesser Good sit on the other diagonal because **both are mismatched**. The Bad Lie does visible work while the benefit runs backwards. The Good Lie claims to be doing nothing while the benefit runs forwards. This is the lie diagonal, marked as the Scale of Nothing.

So truth on this map is not a property of a statement. It is alignment between direction of will and direction of benefit. A lie is a mismatch between the two. That definition is the whole reason the compass doubles as a truth detector.

---

## 3. The centre, and why zero is not neutral

The exact centre is No-one. The centre question printed there is "Who Does this Idea Benefit?"

The critical distinction, and the one people miss most often: **no one is not the same as everyone**. Everyone lives at the far +υ end. No one lives at the origin. An action that helps nobody is not a modest version of an action that helps everybody. It is a different thing entirely, sitting in the dead centre where nothing moves.

Along the 0ψ horizontal, the map runs through a sequence of benefit scopes: Other Beings, Other Being, No-one, Only My Group, Only Me. These are the preference positions, the pure who-benefits reading with no will attached. The left of that line is marked The Good Preference, the right The Bad Preference.

---

## 4. The two rings: ±1 and ±2

The map has an inner horizon and an outer horizon, and they answer different questions.

**±1 is the individual or human horizon.** The objective outcome for real people, ego stripped out. This is where the four named corners live. Most day-to-day judgements resolve at this ring.

**±2 is the systemic or strategic horizon.** The structural effect on societies and institutions. Not "did this person get helped" but "what does the system do differently now."

The outer ring carries its own labels. On the far corners the illustration reads *Take to give to everyone*, *Give to everyone*, *Do nothing*, *Give to me*, *Take to give to me*. The two "take to give" positions are the strategic verbs. They are what the ±1 ring cannot express: the redistributive act, in either direction. Taking to give to everyone is confiscation in the service of a commons. Taking to give to me is extraction. Both are structural moves, and both are invisible if you only score at the human horizon.

An action can score well at ±1 and badly at ±2, or the reverse. That is not a contradiction in the instrument, it is the instrument reporting that a thing is locally kind and structurally corrosive, or locally harsh and structurally sound.

---

## 5. The quadrant names

The four quadrants are named for what they do to the world, not for who does them.

- **Productive** (top left, +υ +ψ). Acting, for everyone. Things get made and the making is shared.
- **Reductive** (top right, -υ +ψ). Acting, for self. Things get made and the making concentrates.
- **Constructive** (bottom left, +υ -ψ). Withholding, for everyone. Nothing is made and the not-making protects.
- **Regressive** (bottom right, -υ -ψ). Withholding, for self. Nothing is made and the not-making takes.

The pairing worth sitting with is Productive against Constructive. Both are on the good side of υ, but one builds and one refrains. The map does not rank them. It just tells you which one you are doing.

---

## 6. The pronoun frames

Four framed statements sit on the top and bottom edges. They are the grammar of the four quadrants, written as the thing people actually say.

**Top left, "You":[me, me] = "For You, but Only for Me."**
The word "you" is spoken, but both slots inside the bracket are *me*. Stated recipient is the other party; actual recipient is the speaker, twice over.

**Top right, "Me":[you, me] = "Me and You, but Really for Me."**
The word "me" is spoken honestly, but a "you" is inserted into the bracket to soften it. The self-interest is admitted and then padded.

**Bottom left, "You":[me, you] = "For You, Me and Everyone."**
The bracket contains both parties. Stated and actual agree. This is the honest inclusive form.

**Bottom right, "Me":[me, you] = "For Me, only Me, and you take what I don't need."**
Fully honest extraction. It states the priority order and then offers the remainder. Nothing is concealed.

The mechanic on display: what a person says is the label outside the colon, what they mean is inside the bracket. The gap between them is the lie, and its size is measurable. This is the same operation as the hypocrisy gap used elsewhere in the framework, expressed in pronouns instead of coordinates.

---

## 7. The edges: Do / Do Not, Optimism / Pessimism

The vertical axis carries a second labelling on the right side: **Do** at the top, **Do Not** at the bottom. That is ψ in its bluntest form. Everything above the midline is a doing, everything below is a not-doing.

The horizontal edges carry **Optimism** at top centre and **Pessimism** at bottom centre. Read against ψ rather than υ, this is a temperament reading: the willingness to act is optimism about outcomes, the refusal to act is pessimism about them. A thermal cue also sits at the top centre of the illustration, consistent with the same hot-above, cold-below reading.

---

## 8. The emotional ring

Layered over the coordinate space is a ring of named emotional zones. Reading clockwise from the top left: Bliss, Alert, Tense, Greed, Anger, Sadness, Depression, Destructive, Bored, Acceptance, Peaceful, Happiness, Motivated, and Confusion at the dead centre.

What this layer asserts is that emotional states are not free-floating. They are *positions*, and they fall out of the same two questions. High action plus wide benefit reads as bliss and motivation. High action plus narrow benefit reads as greed, tension and anger. Low action plus wide benefit reads as peace and acceptance. Low action plus narrow benefit reads as sadness, depression and destruction. The centre, where neither question has an answer, is confusion.

This is a genuine structural claim and not decoration: it says the felt state is a readout of where you are standing, which is why the closing exercise on the diagram works at all.

---

## 9. The paths

Several routes are drawn across the field. Their function is to convert the map from a static scoring grid into a trajectory instrument.

**Path to Enlightenment** and **Path to Truth** run up the productive side toward the Greater Good corner. **The Way** runs alongside them. These are the routes that carry the actor from wherever they are toward alignment.

**Paths to Nowhere** appear repeatedly, and this repetition is the point. There are many of them and they all lead to the same place, the dead centre. Extraction, withholding, self-deception, indifference: different starting positions, same terminus.

The scales printed along the diagonals label what each direction measures. The Scale of Good and Evil and the Scale of the Truth and Progress run along the honest diagonal. The Scale of Nothing and the Scale of Lies and Regression run along the mismatched one.

The practical upshot is that a coordinate on its own is a snapshot, but a coordinate plus a direction of travel is a prediction. Where an actor sits matters less than which way they are moving through the field.

## 10. The Perceptual Inversion Warning

The map carries its own failure mode, printed as a warning.

Near the self-serving end of the υ axis, the ego distorts perception. Specifically:

- Extraction feels like strength.
- Service feels like burden.
- Stability feels like rot.
- Cruelty feels like protection.

When an actor's stated moral position conflicts with the calculated coordinate, that distortion is flagged explicitly rather than being averaged away.

This is the single most important thing about the instrument and the reason it is not merely a rubric. It builds in the observation that **the people who most need the coordinate are the least able to compute their own**. Self-assessment near -υ produces a systematically wrong answer, and the wrongness has a known direction and a known shape. That makes it correctable.

## 11. The trust chain

The footer states a dependency sequence, and it reads as a chain of preconditions rather than a set of aphorisms:

Words are only good as much as they're understood. Senses only work as much as they're understood and trusted. Communication only works as much as priors are open. Societal trust only exists as long as communication is maintained. Progress toward production and construction can only exist with high societal trust. Harmony only exists when progress is achieved.

Read backwards, it is a diagnosis: no harmony means no progress, which means no trust, which means broken communication, which means closed priors, which means shared words have stopped carrying shared meaning. It locates the failure at the bottom of the stack, in the definitions, which is precisely what the subtitle *The Geometry of Definitions* is claiming.

This also explains why the whole diagram lives on the productive-versus-regressive axis rather than a left-right political one. Production and construction are downstream of trust; trust is downstream of definitions holding still. A society that has lost its shared definitions cannot get to the top half of the map regardless of intent.

## 12. The closing exercise

The bottom of the sheet is not summary text, it is a calibration procedure.

First: look at the Greater Good and think Greater Evil, and notice the sense of wrong, a downward tension. Then look at the Greater Evil and think Greater Good, and notice the sense of right, an upward lightening. Then pick a point and ask "am I that?"

What this is doing is checking the reader's own instrument against the map. The felt tension is the emotional ring reporting a mismatch. If the reader feels it, their internal compass agrees with the drawn one and they can trust their own readings. If they feel nothing, the exercise has surfaced that fact before they start scoring anything important.

The questions that follow walk the seven planes in order without naming them: what are your stated and hidden intentions (Q1 will, Q4 meaning), where would you judge them (Q3 position), if they're bad why do you do them (Q5 consistency), consider your tools and what you know (Q2 possibility), consider the cause, what'll be the effect (Q6 and Q7).

## 13. Where the vocabulary comes from

Every term in the header is load-bearing and each has a specific pedigree.

**Phronesis (φρόνησις).** In ancient Greek philosophy this is the wisdom concerned with practical action, implying good judgement and excellence of character, and it is distinguished from other words for wisdom precisely because of its practical character. Aristotle separates it from *sophia* in Book VI of the Nicomachean Ethics: sophia handles universal truths, phronesis handles the particulars that bear on what to actually do. The traditional Latin rendering is *prudentia*, the source of English "prudence", though modern usage prefers "practical wisdom" or "judgement" because prudence has narrowed toward self-interest in a way phronesis never meant.

That is the right word for this instrument. The map does not tell you what is universally true. It tells you what a particular act is doing, right now, to particular people. It is a phronetic tool by construction.

**Hegemonikon (ἡγεμονικόν).** The ruling or commanding part of the soul, responsible for sensation, judgement, impulse and assent. The doctrine was developed most fully by the Stoics of the third century BCE, who held the rational soul to be a single unified hegemonikon located in the heart. In the Stoic account, the senses stretch out from the commanding faculty to gather impressions, and the commanding faculty either assents to them or refuses. The Stoics also theorised a cosmic counterpart in the World-soul, not just a human one.

So "Hegemony" here is not political dominance. It is the ruling faculty, the part that receives impressions and decides. Naming the map after it says plainly what the map is for: it is an aid to the faculty of assent, a way of deciding whether to say yes to an impression.

**Psochic, from psyche (ψυχή).** The Greek word for soul, whose basic meaning was "life"; a derivation from the verb ψύχω, "to blow", is often repeated but is not well supported. It is distinguished from *pneuma*, breath or spirit. Ψ, the letter psi, became the international symbol for psychology.

Which makes the choice of ψ for the will axis exact rather than arbitrary. The vertical axis is literally the psyche axis, the soul's motion, and υ (upsilon) marks the scope of benefit.

**Scales of Judgement.** The weighing of souls, *psychostasia*, is the motif in which a person's life is assessed by weighing their soul immediately before or after death to judge their fate. In Egypt this appears as early as the Old Kingdom and is imagined as a weighing in the Coffin Texts, reaching its best-known form in the New Kingdom Book of the Dead, where the heart is weighed against a feather. Maat is the personification of truth, justice and cosmic order, and the ceremony weighed the heart in a scale balanced by Maat or by her hieroglyph, the ostrich feather, as a test of conformity to proper values. In its abstract sense, *maat* was the divine order set at creation, established in place of *isfet*, disorder.

The borrowed structure is precise: a heart, a fixed standard, and a balance. The Hegemony replaces the feather with a coordinate but keeps the operation. The heart is not measured against another heart, it is measured against a standard that does not move.

**Seal of Solomon.** The hexagram, two superimposed equilateral triangles, was used by Muslims as a mystic symbol in the medieval period under this name, depicted as either a hexagram or a pentagram, and appeared as a decorative motif in both medieval Christian churches and Jewish synagogues. In esoteric readings the interlocking triangles stand for the union of opposites, and the upward triangle for aspiration while the downward one stands for revelation descending into the material.

That is the same up-and-down structure as ψ. Two opposed motions, one ascending and one descending, locked into a single figure. The watermark is not ornament, it is the axis restated as a seal.

## 14. What the map is not

It is worth naming the neighbours, because people will pattern-match to them and then misread the instrument.

**It is not a political compass.** Two-axis political maps have a long history: Eysenck argued in *The Psychology of Politics* (1954) for reconciling contradictory placements by accepting a two-dimensional system with a left-right abscissa and a democratic-versus-autocratic ordinate; Bryson and McDill published a bi-dimensional approach in 1968; David Nolan's chart followed in 1969 using economic freedom against personal freedom; Christie and Meltzer's 1970 version used collectivism-capitalism against individualism-totalitarianism. All of these plot *ideologies*. The Hegemony plots *acts*. A single person will occupy many different points on it in a single day, which is not true of a political compass and is the clearest tell that they are different instruments.

**It is not a mood chart.** Russell's circumplex model organises affect along valence and arousal, and Plutchik's wheel arranges eight primary emotions with opposites 180 degrees apart and intensity varying by distance from centre. The Hegemony's emotional ring resembles both, but the causation is reversed. In the circumplex, position *is* the emotion. Here, position is a moral fact and the emotion is a *consequence* of standing there. The ring is an output, not the coordinate system.

**It is not a semiotic square, though it is close.** Greimas's square, derived from Aristotle's square of opposition, expands a binary opposition into four terms related by contrariety, contradiction and implication, and Greimas considered it the elementary structure of meaning. The Hegemony's four corners have exactly this shape: Greater Good and Greater Evil are contraries, and the diagonal pairs are contradictories. The difference is that the semiotic square is discrete and unoriented, a taxonomy of relations. The Hegemony is continuous and scored. You can sit at (0.4, -1.7) on it, and you cannot sit between two vertices of a semiotic square.

The lineage is real and worth owning. The novelty is not the four-corner structure, which is ancient. The novelty is putting *who benefits* and *what the will does* on the two axes, which produces the truth-and-lie diagonal as a by-product rather than as a separate assertion.

## 15. Why machines read it instantly and people do not

Both facts are true and they have the same cause.

A machine reads the map easily because the map is a coordinate assignment with two clearly defined dimensions, explicit anchors, and a stated output format. It asks for a measurement, and measurement against fixed axes is a native operation. It also does not ask the reader to be anything, which means there is no ego to distort the reading. Handing it to a model does not so much install a personality as centre the model on the information: it replaces "what should I say about this" with "where does this sit."

A person struggles because the map refuses the question they came to ask. Most people have been trained to judge the *actor*, not the *vector*. "Is this person good" is a comfortable question with a social answer. "Which way is this benefit moving" is neither. The map declines the first question entirely, and that refusal reads as a trick to anyone who has only ever played the first game.

The Perceptual Inversion Warning covers the rest. The further toward -υ someone sits, the more extraction reads to them as competence, which means self-scoring fails exactly where it matters most.

There is also an honest presentational problem. The explainer sheet carries at least six overlapping systems at once: the coordinate grid, the four corners, the emotional ring, the paths, the scales, the pronoun frames, and the elemental watermarks. The core is two questions and four corners. The sheet is not two questions and four corners. Everything on it earns its place once the core has landed, and almost none of it earns its place before.

## 16. Known inconsistencies to resolve

Two things on the current sheet want a decision.

**The -1υ, +1ψ corner has two names.** The illustration labels it *Lesser Evil / The Bad Lie*. The formal coordinate protocol names the same corner *Greatest Lie*. Both readings are defensible: "Lesser Evil" describes its position relative to Greater Evil in the moral ordering, while "Greatest Lie" describes its position on the deception diagonal. They should not both be in circulation without a note saying which is primary.

**The υ axis is drawn mirrored.** On the sheet, +2υ sits at the far *left* and -2υ at the far right, so benefit-to-everyone is on the left and benefit-to-self on the right. This is internally consistent throughout the diagram, but it inverts the convention every reader brings from Cartesian graphs, where positive x runs right. It costs the first-time reader several seconds of silent recalibration at exactly the moment they are least able to spare it.

## 17. The minimum viable version

For handing to someone cold, everything above reduces to this:

A blank cross. Four words in the corners. Two questions written on the axes.

- Horizontal: *who does this benefit?* Everyone on one end, only me on the other, no one in the middle.
- Vertical: *is the will doing something, or refusing to?*
- Top left corner: Greater Good. Top right: Lesser Evil. Bottom left: Lesser Good. Bottom right: Greater Evil.
- One sentence underneath: the two honest corners are diagonal to each other, and so are the two lies.

Everything else, the zones, the paths, the scales, the seals, the pronoun frames, is sheet two.
