# Omni-Weave Bigram Reference Table

Standardised definitions for phonetic bigrams used in Omni-Weave runs. Each bigram carries fixed cross-language definitions regardless of which word it appears in. Context-sensitivity enters only at Layer 1 and above. No compressions -- each bigram stands on its own as a unit, not as the beginning of a longer word.

Entries marked ⚠ need cross-language review before being treated as settled.

---

## Rules

- Definitions are locked per bigram. The same bigram gets the same definitions in every weave run.
- If a longer word starts with these two letters, that is irrelevant unless the two-letter form is itself a genuine standalone root.
- Doubling (DD, NN etc) = intensification/total closure of the preceding unit. Not a language-specific meaning -- a structural one.
- When definitions across languages genuinely cluster, note the cluster. When they scatter, hold the scatter as ambiguity and note it explicitly.
- Entries are held in strict alphabetical order. New entries are inserted in place, never appended to the end of the table. Hyphen-prefixed suffix entries sort on their letters (-LY sorts as LY).

## Table

**AA**
Low Franconian and Dutch Aa = river, running water; a widespread hydronym, from Proto-Germanic *ahwo (running water), from PIE *h2ekweh2 (moving water); cognate with Old High German aha, Old Norse a, Gothic ahwa, all meaning river [100%]
Structural: also the doubled vowel, which in most orthographies marks length rather than a separate unit (Dutch aa, Finnish aa, Estonian aa).
Cluster: running water, and lengthening. Where AA appears in a name of Germanic or Low Countries origin the river reading is live; elsewhere treat it as the held vowel.

**AB**

Cluster: origin-departure AND origin-source. Away-from and the father/ancestor it departs from are the same phoneme.

**AC**

Two genuine non-converging clusters: sharpness (Greek/Latin) · connective-restriction (Hebrew/OE). Hold both.

**AD**
Hebrew ad (עַד) = until/eternity/perpetuity [100%] · Latin ad = toward/at [100%] · PIE *ad = at/near [100%] · Old Norse at = to/toward [90% -- t/d minimal voicing] · Welsh at = to/toward [90% -- t/d minimal voicing] · Sanskrit adhi = upon/over/toward [85% -- adhi vs ad]
Cluster: directional approach meeting perpetuity. Toward, and toward forever. Confirmed across Semitic, IE, and Celtic.

**AE**

Cluster: law, permanence, and vital force. The enduring material and the oath that lasts.

**AF**
Hebrew af = nose, and by the standing Hebraism, anger; the idiom charon af, the burning of the nose, is the ordinary biblical phrase for wrath [100%] . Hebrew af also = also, even, indeed [90%]
Cluster: the nose as the seat of anger. AF is the breathing part of the face doing double duty as the emotion that flares through it. The additive sense (also, even) sits alongside without converging -- hold it separately.

**AG**
Irish ag = at; the preposition that also forms the progressive, and that combines with pronouns (ag + me becomes agam, at-me, which is how Irish says "have") [100%] . PIE *ag- = to drive, to lead, to act [90% -- reconstructed root, no standalone attestation]
Cluster: position and driving. The Irish ag marks where something is and, in the progressive, that it is under way. Thin on citations; treat as provisional.

**AH**
Hebrew ah (אָח) = brother [100%] · Arabic ah = sigh/release [100%] · Sanskrit ah = to say/speak (verbal root) [100%] · German ach = oh/alas [90% -- ach vs ah] · Yoruba àh = exclamation of surprise [100%] · Bengali ah = sigh/oh [100%] · raw open vowel = unobstructed breath, maximum aperture [inherent]
Cluster: openness and release. The unobstructed. AH is the most universal human breath-sound -- across unrelated families it marks the open expulsion of breath at moments of recognition, kinship, or release.

**AI**

Cluster: love and lament in the same phoneme. AI is both the grief-cry and the love. The going-toward and the woe of arrival.

**AK**

Cluster: whiteness, flowing, and the adversative. The command to flow and the word for "but" share the phoneme, as do white, clean and honest. Note the overlap with the AC entry's connective-restriction cluster -- AK and AC hold the same "but" from two spellings.

**AN**

**AO**
Japanese ao (青) = the undivided blue-green band; historically the only term covering that range before midori entered for plant-green, and still the word used for a green traffic light [100%] . Vietnamese ao = pond [100%] . Vietnamese áo = bay, inlet, a non-Sino-Vietnamese reading of Chinese 澳; compare Thai aao and Khmer aav [100%] . Vietnamese áo = upper garment, shirt, from Proto-Bahnaric *ʔa:w, from Proto-Mon-Khmer *ʔaawʔ (upper garment); cognates Khmer aau, Muong ảo [100%] . Hawaiian ao = light, day, dawn, world; also cloud [100%] . Maori ao = world, daytime, dawn, cloud [100%]
Two clusters.
(1) The blue-green water band: the Japanese colour term for the range that covers sky and water without dividing them, beside the Vietnamese pond, the Vietnamese and Thai bay or inlet. The colour and the water-body occupy the same sound across two unrelated families.
(2) Polynesian light, day, dawn, world and cloud. This touches (1) at the dawn sky, which is the ao colour, but the families are unrelated and the contact should be noted rather than asserted.
The Mon-Khmer upper garment is a third line converging with neither. Hold it separately.

**AP**
Welsh ap = son, from Old Welsh map, Welsh mab; the patronymic particle in Welsh names [100%] . PIE *ap- = water, behind Persian ab (water) and related forms [100%] . PIE *apo- = away, off [100%] . Greek ap = without, as in ap ommaton, without eyes [100%]
Cluster: descent and water, with away-ness alongside. AP is one of the denser vowel-consonant units: the Welsh patronymic names the line a person comes out of, the PIE root names flowing water, and *apo- names departure. Coming-from is the thread that holds all three; the Greek privative sits apart.

**AT**

**AV**

**AX**
No standalone attestation found. English axe and Old English aex are whole words beginning with the sequence, not attestations of it. Structural: record as unattested.

**AY**
Turkish, Azerbaijani, Gagauz, Crimean Tatar and Turkmen ay = moon/month, inherited from Proto-Turkic *ay [100%] . Middle English ay = always/ever/continually/eternally, from Old Norse ei, from Proto-Germanic *aiwaz (eternity, age), from PIE *h2eyu- (vitality); cognate with Ancient Greek aei = always and Latin aevum = an age [100%] . Scots ay = yes [100%] . Spanish ay and Turkish ay = cry of pain or surprise [100%] . Tocharian A and B ay = bone [90%] . Somali ay = dog [90%]
Cluster: recurrence and duration. The Turkic moon-month is the returning body; the Germanic ay is perpetuity, and its PIE root is vitality itself. Two unrelated families put the thing that comes back and the thing that never ends in the same phoneme. The Scots assent and the Iberian pain-cry sit alongside without converging -- hold the scatter.
Negative finding, recorded because it was tested: AY does not attest anywhere as island, land, water or bounded place. Old English ieg (island), which survives orthographically in the -sey / -say / -ay of Lindsey and Lindsay, is a spelling descent and not a citation for this bigram. Any weave reading AY as island is importing the orthography.

**AZ**

Cluster: none, and the scatter is worth noting precisely because it is wide. The Slavic first person, the Persian ablative, the Turkic diminisher and the Hungarian demonstrative are four unrelated families with four unrelated senses. AZ carries I, from, few and that. Hold all four; force nothing.

**BA**
Hebrew ba (בָּא) = coming/entering [100%] · Arabic ba = house/within [100%] · Sanskrit ba = binding force [100%] · Mandarin ba (吧) = suggestion/question particle -- the entering-into-assertion [100%] · Japanese ba (場) = place/location/the-space-entered [100%] · Korean ba = basis/foundation [90%]
Cluster: inward arrival. The entering that binds. The place arrived at.

**BB**
Structural: orthographic doubling of B. Unlike LL in Welsh and RR in Basque, BB is not a distinct phoneme in any language surveyed -- in English and most Romance orthographies it marks the shortness of the preceding vowel rather than a separate sound. Treat as intensification of the preceding unit, and note that the intensification is weaker than the LL and RR cases because no language phonemically distinguishes it.

**BI**

Cluster: none. Two, I, and seed across three unrelated families with no shared thread. The Latin bi- (two) that an English reader expects is the same sense as the Basque but reaches it independently, which is worth noting without treating it as convergence. Hold the scatter.

**BO**

**BU**

**CC**
Structural: orthographic doubling of C. As with BB, not a distinct phoneme anywhere surveyed; in Italian it marks gemination and in English it marks a sound change (accent, succeed) rather than intensity. Treat as weak intensification and prefer to slice around it where a name allows.

**CE**

**CH**

**CI**

Cluster: thin. The Italian is a first-person-plural and locative particle; the Turkic is an occupational former. What they share is that both attach rather than stand, which is a grammatical observation rather than a semantic one. Under-attested; treat any CI reading as weak.

**CO**

**CU**

**DD**

**DE**

**DS**
Structural: consonant cluster, the voiced sibilant stop-into-fricative — English plural/possessive marker (-'s, -s). No standalone cross-language citation found beyond this grammatical function.

**EA**

Cite it in full, carry it in the layers, and discount it in the convergence.

**HM**
Sound-symbolic: the closed-mouth nasal hum, the sound of held consideration. No language-specific citation; it functions cross-linguistically as breath held with the mouth shut. Structurally the inverse of AH, which is breath released at maximum aperture.
