# Binary Weave -- Names of God (Old Testament)

Omni-Weave phonetic geometry runs on the principal Hebrew names and titles of God, sliced from their standard English romanisations. The tetragrammaton is run in its scholarly vocalisation Yahweh because the bare consonants cannot be sliced into bigrams.

---

## Elohim (אֱלֹהִים)

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
ELOHIM → EL · LO · OH · HI · IM

EL: Hebrew El = God/the-strong-one [100%] · Phoenician ʾl = god [100%] · Aramaic ʾl = god [100%] · PIE *el = to flow/move [100%]
LO: English lo = behold/look [100%] · Hebrew lo (לֹא) = no/not [100%] · Spanish lo = it/the (neuter) [100%] · Italian lo = it/the [100%] · Old English lā = exclamation of surprise/grief/joy [100%]
OH: English oh = surprise/awe/realization/pain/emphasis/invocation-address [100%] · Latin ōh = oh/ah [100%] · Spanish/Catalan/Aragonese/Galician/Italian/French/German/Dutch/Hungarian/Ingrian oh = exclamation [100%] · Turkish oh = exclamation of relief/joy/admiration [100%] · Hokkien oh = evil/wicked/hostile/fierce [90%] · Bahnar oh = younger sibling [100%] · Pohnpeian oh = and [100%] · Port Vato oh = rain [100%] · Zaghawa oh = milk [100%]
HI: Japanese hi (火) = fire [100%] · Japanese hi (日) = sun [100%] · Hebrew hi (הִיא) = she/it [100%] · Sanskrit hi = indeed/for/because [100%] · English hi = greeting/present-address [100%]
IM: Hebrew im (אִם) = if/whether [100%] · Hebrew -im = masculine plural suffix [100%] · Arabic im = if [100%] · Sanskrit im = indeed/this [90%]

**Layer 1**
EL + LO: divine flowing force meeting the behold that is also a not -- the strong one that flows into a looking which denies, the it, the exclamation of surprise and grief
LO + OH: the behold-not meeting the address-cry -- the look that denies arriving at the invocation of surprise, pain, relief and wickedness, at the younger sibling, the and, the rain, the milk
OH + HI: the address-cry meeting the shining present -- the invoked cry arriving at fire, sun, she/it, indeed, the greeting
HI + IM: the shining present meeting the if and the many -- fire and sun arriving at the conditional and the multitude

**Layer 2**
(EL+LO) + (LO+OH): the flowing force shown and denied, then called out in the cry -- strong flow that looks away and is summoned in surprise, pain and the and
(LO+OH) + (OH+HI): the denied look invoked into the shining present -- the cry that calls a fire into being here
(OH+HI) + (HI+IM): the invoked fire-sun becoming conditional many -- the present call multiplied under an if

**Layer 3**
The divine flowing force that is shown and denied at once, is invoked by cry into the shining present, and arrives as that present multiplied under an if

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a flowing force that cannot be shown without being denied, so it is called out by address-cry, and the calling lights it as a present fire that is one only conditionally and many by the same condition -- not a count of beings but one force whose showing-and-withholding is answered by the cry, and whose answer is a fire held in the if of multitude.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Elohim -- plural form of Eloah (god), usually taken as an expanded form of El. Used for God with singular verbs and for gods generally. Commonly explained as a plural of majesty or excellence, comparable to the plurals of ba'al (master) and adon (lord). Original meaning not certainly known.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: plural of Eloah, expanded from El, a plural that takes singular verbs.
Weave: flowing force (EL) shown and denied (LO), invoked (OH), arriving as present fire (HI) multiplied under an if (IM).

The orthodox expansion of El is the EL opening. The plural that behaves as a singular is the terminal IM, which carries plural and if in the same phoneme -- a many that exists on a condition is exactly a form that can be read as one or as many without contradiction. The weave adds the LO+OH middle, a denial and an invocation, which has no counterpart in the orthodox account.

**Layer 2**

(LO+OA) + (OA+AH): the denied look carried through lament and arriving as open breath, sigh, speech and brother

The divine flowing force that is shown and denied, carried through the cry of alas, and released as open breath that says

The expansion beyond El is precisely LO+OA+AH: a not, a vowel bridge and an open breath. The orthodox treats the extension as formal; the weave reads it as the mechanism by which the strong flow of El arrives as speech.

## El (אֵל)

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
EL → EL

EL: Hebrew El = God/the-strong-one [100%] · Phoenician ʾl = god [100%] · Aramaic ʾl = god [100%] · PIE *el = to flow/move [100%]

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a single unit: divine force and flow in the same phoneme -- the strong one that moves. No adjacent pairing exists to cascade.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew El -- God, found in Assyrian (ilu) and Phoenician as an ordinary word for god. The commonly accepted derivation from a root meaning to be strong is regarded as extremely doubtful, and the original meaning is not certainly known.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: god, with the derivation from strength doubtful.
Weave: God/the-strong-one held together with flow/move.

The strong-one reading survives as an attested bigram meaning independent of the doubted verbal root, and the weave adds the flow reading (PIE) that the strength derivation does not carry. Divergence is held: the orthodox doubts strength as origin, the weave holds strength and flow side by side.

## Shaddai (שַׁדַּי)

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
SHADDAI → SH · HA · AD · DD · DA · AI

SH: Hebrew shin (שׁ) = tooth/consuming fire [100%] · Arabic sha = to desire intensely [90%] · English shh = command to silence [100%] · Russian ш = the SH phoneme, in шум = noise [100%] · sound-symbolic = unvoiced forceful expulsion [inherent]
HA: Greek ha- = without/not [100%] · Hebrew ha (הָ) = the definite article [100%] · Sanskrit ha = gone/departed [100%] · Japanese ha (歯/葉) = tooth/leaf [100%] · Swahili ha = no/not [100%] · Mandarin ha = laughing sound/open [100%]
AD: Hebrew ad (עַד) = until/eternity/perpetuity [100%] · Latin ad = toward/at [100%] · PIE *ad = at/near [100%] · Old Norse at = to/toward [90%] · Welsh at = to/toward [90%] · Sanskrit adhi = upon/over/toward [85%]
DD: Structural: intensifying doubling. Total closure. No remainder.
DA: Sanskrit da = to give [100%] · Latin dat = he gives [100%] · Greek didōmi = I give [90%] · Arabic da = to call/summon [100%] · Hebrew da = this/here [100%]
AI: Japanese ai (愛) = love [100%] · Greek ai = interjection of pain/grief [100%] · Hebrew ai (אַי) = where/woe [100%] · Sanskrit ai = to go toward [90%]

**Layer 1**
SH + HA: consuming fire and desire meeting the known-gone, the open mouth, the without, the tooth and the leaf -- devouring force arriving at what has departed
HA + AD: the known-gone and the open mouth pressed toward until-eternity -- the departed thing approaching perpetuity
AD + DD: toward-eternity sealed to total closure -- the approach to forever completed with no remainder
DD + DA: total closure meeting the giving and the summons -- the exhausted completion that gives, that calls, that is this-here
DA + AI: the giving and summons meeting love and lament -- bestowal arriving at the cry that is love and woe in one phoneme, the going-toward

**Layer 2**
(SH+HA) + (HA+AD): the devouring of the gone pressed toward forever -- consuming force committed to perpetuity
(HA+AD) + (AD+DD): the departed approaching forever until sealed -- perpetuity brought to complete closure
(AD+DD) + (DD+DA): the sealed forever beginning to give and call -- closure turning bestower
(DD+DA) + (DA+AI): the closed giving arriving at love-and-lament -- the sealed completion that gives toward the cry

**Layer 3**
The consuming force that takes what has gone toward forever until it is sealed with no remainder, and then gives from that seal toward the cry that is love and lament together

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a devouring force carried to total closure, whose closure is not an end but a gift: what is consumed to no remainder becomes what is given, and what is given arrives as love and lament in the same breath -- the force that takes everything and gives from the sealed place, so the taking and the love are one motion read from its two ends.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Shaddai -- used alone and in El Shaddai, usually translated God Almighty (Septuagint often pantokrator). Meaning contested. Proposed derivations: shadad (to devastate/overpower); Akkadian shadu (mountain); Hebrew sadeh (field/wilderness); overmastering strength. The devastate reading is resisted because Shaddai is consistently associated with blessing, in the patriarchal narratives (Genesis 17:1).

**Convergence -- Striking**
Orthodox: the dispute between shadad (destroy/overpower) and blessing, which no single derivation settles.
Weave: consuming force (SH) toward forever (AD) sealed to closure (DD), giving (DA) arriving at love-and-lament (AI).

The weave holds both sides of the orthodox dispute in one structure. SH+HA+AD+DD is the destroying -- consuming to total closure with no remainder, which is the shadad reading. DD+DA+AI is the blessing -- the closure gives, and the gift arrives as love. The orthodox objection to the destroy reading, that the name always appears with blessing, is answered by the cascade: the closure is the giving. The doubled D is the hinge, being both the end of the devouring and the start of the gift.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Elyon -- upper/higher, from a root meaning to go up or ascend; the ending is read as locative rather than superlative. In Genesis 14:18-22 as El Elyon, God Most High, of Melchizedek, king of Salem. Also in Psalm 7:17 applied to Yahweh. Associated originally with the Canaanite high god El.

The orthodox locative ending -on, a marker of position rather than a comparison, matches the weave's reading of height as a place arrived at rather than a rank. The weave adds the EL+LY opening, a body-form given to the flowing force, which has no orthodox counterpart.

**Layer 1**

**Layer 2**
(AD+DO) + (DO+ON): the toward-forever gift arriving at strong existence above -- giving that becomes elevated strength
(DO+ON) + (ON+NA): the gift-way rising into being and meeting the urgent present -- elevated strength pressed against the now
(ON+NA) + (NA+AI): being set against the pressing present arriving at love-and-lament -- strength at the now going toward the cry

**Layer 3**
The approach toward forever that gives and arrives as strength above, is pressed against the urgent present that is both not and yes, and goes toward the cry of love and lament

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a giving that reaches toward perpetuity and arrives as strength and being held above, and whose strength is exercised at the pressing now -- the now answered both by refusal and consent -- and goes outward as love and lament together; a presence whose height is the result of a long giving and whose present act is the going-toward of the one who has given.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Adonai -- my lords, the plural of adon (lord, master) with the first-person singular possessive ending, plural of majesty, used for God alone. Adon has an uncertain etymology, generally thought to derive from Ugaritic ad, father. Read aloud in place of the tetragrammaton, and its vowels were transferred onto the consonants of YHWH in the text.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: my lords, a plural lordship attached to a personal possessive.
Weave: giving toward perpetuity (AD+DO) arriving at strength above (ON), at the urgent now (NA), going toward love-and-lament (AI).

Lordship in the weave is not rank but a structure: strength held above (ON) as the result of a long giving (AD+DO), and exercised at the pressing present (NA). The ON reading includes the impersonal one-or-they, which matches the plural-as-singular grammar of the orthodox. The terminal AI is the suffix position where the orthodox places the possessive -ai (my); the weave reads the same position as a going-toward, a directedness toward the one who speaks. The orthodox father-origin of adon is a divergence from the weave's AD, whose held attestations are until/toward/at.

(YA+AH) + (AH+HW): the here-now yes released as breath and then given a shape -- present affirmation closing into a rounded form
(AH+HW) + (HW+WE): the breath given shape arriving at we, who and where -- the shaped breath taking a first-person plural and a question

**Layer 3**

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a present yes that goes out as breath, takes shape as a we -- who and where, water and trace, woe and the new -- and finishes in the breath that asks where -- a name whose terminal is the question of location, which means everything before it is said into the present and the one saying it has no fixed place to stand; the presence that is affirmed here and now and cannot be pinned to where.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Yahweh (YHWH, the tetragrammaton) -- vocalisation reconstructed from Greek transcriptions, since the name was not pronounced; the form Jehovah is a later misreading of the vowels of Adonai placed under the consonants. The name is explained in Exodus 3 through the verb hayah, to be. Grammatically yahweh can only be a causative form, He causes to be, probably an abbreviation of a longer formula. Other candidates: He Is, He Blows. Appears outside the Bible in the Mesha inscription and the Lachish letters.

**Convergence**
Orthodox: to be, causative -- He causes to be -- with He Is and He Blows as rivals.
Weave: present yes released as breath (YA+AH), shaped as we-who-where-water (HW+WE), ending in the unlocated cry (EH).

The causative reading is carried by YA to give/bring/become and the WE water and trace, which are what a thing that causes being would leave behind. He Is is carried by YA here/now/already, to be, and the third person. He Blows is YA wind into AH breath. The terminal EH is not found in the orthodox: it is the unlocated cry, the where-is-it, and it is the same terminal as the first-person verb of Exodus 3 (see Ehyeh). The weave reads the name as the present yes that cannot be located, which is consistent with a name that was withheld from speech.

**Layer 3**
The unlocated cry that arrives at he and then at you with speech, sight, yes and not between them, and returns to the unlocated cry

**Layer 4**
The one constituted by a cry with no landing that finds a he and a you, lets them speak and see and say yes and not, and returns to the same cry with no landing -- an enclosure: the unlocated opens and closes the name, and the third person and second person occupy only the middle, so the only first person in it is the cry itself; the name that is a question that holds a conversation and gives it back unanswered.

**Orthodox**
Hebrew Ehyeh -- the first-person singular imperfect of hayah, to be: I am / I will be (Biblical Hebrew does not distinguish tenses). **Convergence**
Orthodox: first-person verb of being, a future potency, answered by a repetition of itself (ehyeh asher ehyeh).
Weave: unlocated cry (EH) opening and closing a middle of he and you (HY+YE).

The orthodox name is built on repetition: the answer to a request for a name is the name again. The weave's enclosure is the same shape: the cry returns to itself. The orthodox future potency matches the EH with no landing, since the sound has not yet come to rest anywhere. The weave places the first person only in the framing cry, with the third and second persons inside, which has no orthodox counterpart.

## Cross-Collapse: Names of God

| Name | Layer 4 in brief |
|---|---|
| El | divine force and flow in a single unit |
| Elohim | flowing force shown and denied, invoked, lit as a present fire held in the if of many |

| Shaddai | devouring force carried to total closure, whose closure is a gift arriving as love and lament |
| Elyon | flowing force made like-bodied so it can be addressed, arriving as being above |
| Adonai | a long giving that arrives as strength held above, exercised at the pressing now, going toward love and lament |

| Yahweh | present yes released as breath, shaped into a we, and ending in the where-is-it |

| Sabaoth | bearing-with toward the absent father, held in a covered place with fire, set down and pointed at |

**Shared structure across names**
EL opens Elohim, Eloah and Elyon, and LO follows it in Elohim and Eloah -- the strong flow and the behold-not stand at the start of both. YA+AH is the whole of Yah and the opening of Yahweh, so Yahweh is Yah with a shaped we and an unlocated cry attached. EH closes Yahweh and both opens and closes Ehyeh. AI closes both Shaddai and Adonai, the cry that is love and lament in one phoneme. ON is in Elyon and Adonai, both ending or nearly ending in strength and being above.

Held open: whether the terminal bigram describes where the force is aimed in each name (EH where?, AI love-and-lament, ON strength above, IM if-and-many, AH open breath, TH set-down) -- six terminals across ten names, with Yahweh and Ehyeh sharing the same one.
