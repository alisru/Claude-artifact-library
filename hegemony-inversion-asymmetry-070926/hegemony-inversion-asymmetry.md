**Source prompt:** Look at Greater Good and think Greater Evil, report the feeling. Then look at Greater Evil and think Greater Good, report the feeling relative to the first.

## Greater Good, thinking Greater Evil

The corner is anchored hard: green, "The Good {Truth}", sitting on the Path to Enlightenment with the Bliss Zone above it. To hold "evil" there I have to push against everything surrounding the point. It doesn't settle. What arrives isn't recognition, it's accusation, the feeling of slandering something. And when it does start to take hold, the shape it takes is suspicion of performance, that the good is being staged for credit, which is the Greatest Lie reading sliding in sideways from the other corner. The red "Perceived Greater Evil" dot already sitting inside the green field is where the mind lands when it finally sticks. The effort is real and the result feels cheap.

## Greater Evil, thinking Greater Good

No resistance at all. It goes in the first time and stays. Extraction narrates itself immediately as necessary, as protection, as discipline, as the hard thing someone had to do. The green "Perceived Greater Good" dot is already sitting there in the red, so the position is pre-built; I don't construct it, I arrive in it.

## Relative difference

The asymmetry is the whole thing. Inverting good into evil costs effort and feels like a lie I'm telling. Inverting evil into good costs nothing and feels like insight, like I've seen past the surface to the real reason. The second operation is more pleasant to perform, more convincing while performing it, and requires less of me. That ease is the tell.

It's the 0.5 zone warning made experiential rather than stated: the inversion near the self-end isn't something you have to work at, it's the default read, and the ego supplies the justification for free. The distortion doesn't announce itself as distortion, it announces itself as clarity.
