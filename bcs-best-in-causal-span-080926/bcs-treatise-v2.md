# Best in Causal Span (BCS)

## A Treatise on Causality, Limits, and Human Action

Jarrod Hamilton (alisru)
September 2026

---

## Abstract

This paper presents Best in Causal Span (BCS), an objective account of causality, material limits, and human action across time. BCS holds that any new entity, event, or decision can only be assembled from the exact physical materials, operational tools, and information present in its immediate prior state.

Each of those three inputs is itself a made thing, produced by an earlier state and limited by it. The paper establishes that this holds not only for matter but on every kind of state a thing can be in: identity, potential, location, meaning, logic, history and consequence. On each of these, nothing jumps. Every change is a continuous translation through intermediate states, driven by force supplied by a tool that is itself finite and had to be built.

From this follow the origin of the universe as the one state with nothing before it, why historical actors could not have acted on knowledge they did not hold, why suffering is a structural feature of sequential time rather than a defect in it, the exact boundary of forgiveness, and why mistakes are real while regret is not.

---

## 1. The Rule

Anything new can only be assembled from what is already present in the immediate prior state.

Every event and every decision is the exact causal output of that state. Not approximately, and not mostly. Exactly, because the state is what the output is made of.

In BCS, "best" does not mean morally approved, well-executed, or good. It names an exact causal resultant. An outcome is the best in its causal span because, given the precise configuration of matter, tools, conditioning and information present at that instant, no other co-available output could have occurred.

You can only use what is from what was to make what will be.

### 1.1 Causality Is Information Moving Forward

A past state is complete. Nothing can be added to it, because everything it was going to produce has been produced.

The present is where assembly happens, and it is the only position where a state is incomplete and something is being done anyway.

Causality is the flow between them. What a completed state holds becomes the material of the state being assembled now, and that one in turn becomes material for the next. There is no other mechanism, and nothing enters the flow from outside it.

### 1.2 What BCS Is For

BCS is not a system of moral judgment and does not sort blame into categories. It is a statement about the mechanics of causality.

Its use is to eliminate meaningless counterfactual claims, of which the clearest instance is "the eleventh century should have had nuclear power."

Every claim of this type needs an alternative to compare against. Specifying that alternative means introducing materials, tools or knowledge that were not present, which changes the state, which means the critic is no longer evaluating this reality but an invented one with an unrelated causal lineage.

Alternative history serves fiction well. Treated as analysis it halts discourse, and it terminates in the same place every time, which is a complaint about the origin of the universe itself. Section 6 treats that terminus.

BCS looks at the past and says: what happened was causally bound to occur exactly as it did.

## 2. The Three Inputs

The assembling state is not a pile of atoms. Three kinds of thing are in it, and each is itself made and therefore limited.

### 2.1 Materials

Matter and energy already present in the local state. Nothing enters from outside it.

Every physical entity also occupies space, consumes energy and excludes alternatives. Two bodies cannot occupy one coordinate. To build, sustain or occupy anything is to draw on finite resources thereby made unavailable to other configurations.

### 2.2 Tools

A tool is a made thing, so the rule applies to it exactly as it applies to anything else.

Anything usable as a tool is of the universe to begin with. There is no lever standing outside a state, and no instrument that was not produced by an earlier one. Every means of changing a state is already inside the state being changed.

An operation is a tool. To reach the number 1, an actor must start at 0, accumulate through intermediate values like 0.1, and invent addition. Zero and addition are not free-standing facts waiting in empty space. They had to be built. An actor holding all the raw material for a machine but lacking the procedure that combines it is as constrained as one holding no material at all.

Section 4 treats tools in full, because they are what supplies force, and force is what every change requires.

### 2.3 Information

An actor's knowledge at any moment is bounded by the inputs, conditioning and history accumulated up to it. No actor can step outside their information state in order to act on a better one, because acquiring information is itself an act, requiring prior tools and time.

Information is an output of prior states, exactly like iron or stone.

This includes individuation. An entity or problem cannot be aimed at, solved or manipulated until it has been picked out and recognised as a distinct thing, and picking out is work done with a tool.

## 3. The Seven Bodies

The rule is not a rule about matter. It governs every kind of state a thing can be in.

A thing has seven bodies, and on each of them the same constraint holds: no state is reachable from another without passing through every intermediate state, and passing through requires force applied by that body's own tool. Without the force, the state stays where it is.

Nothing teleports. Not on any body.

Under the 42-structure the seven comprise one emergent driver and three paired axes.

### 3.1 The Driver

**Who. Identity and will.** An actor cannot jump from one state of identity or will to another. Becoming someone who acts differently means translating through intermediate conditioning, driven by effort, necessity or environmental pressure.

Who drives without being exempt. It sets direction, and it is subject to the same constraint as everything it directs.

### 3.2 The Lateral Axis: Definition and Space

**What. Potential and individuation.** A thing cannot leap to a higher measure of potential. It translates through intermediate thresholds of capacity, driven by trial, discovery or energetic input.

**Where. Location and matter.** Matter cannot jump between coordinates and must occupy every point between, driven by physical work.

### 3.3 The Longitudinal Axis: Function and Meaning

**How. Logic and operations.** A procedure cannot leapfrog its own steps. Progressing through an operation requires computational or physical execution.

**Why. Meaning and resonance.** Meaning does not appear spontaneously. A motive translates through intermediate states of relevance, driven by lived friction and accumulated experience. An act with no available meaning in its setting is not a live option there, however physically possible it is.

### 3.4 The Vertical Axis: Time and Consequence

**Cause. History and precedence.** A downstream state cannot exist without the unbroken chain of states preceding it.

**Effect. Emotion and consequence.** Emotional and physiological states cannot jump. They translate through intermediate states, driven by time, regulation and processing.

### 3.5 What Comes First

The bodies are not independent. Five of them hold attributes of an entity, and What names the entity. Until the naming is done, the rest have nothing to be about. Where is what. How does what work. Why did what happen.

So What is prior, and the priority is a dependency, not a schedule. Nothing here claims Where must be settled before Why, or How before Cause. Those can be worked in any order. The claim is narrower: nothing binds until What is fixed.

Data can be gathered ahead of the What. Measurements, anomalies and correlations pile up for years. But they attach to nothing until the entity is individuated.

**A false What.** In 1846 Le Verrier noticed Uranus deviating from its calculated Newtonian orbit. He defined the What as an unseen outer planet, calculated its Where, and sent the coordinates to Johann Galle, who found Neptune within one degree of the predicted position on the first night, 23 September 1846. In 1859 he observed an anomaly in Mercury's perihelion, assumed the What was again an undiscovered planet, and named it Vulcan. Astronomers searched the calculated Where for decades and found nothing. The method did not fail. A Where computed from a false What is empty rather than merely inaccurate.

**An absent What.** Semmelweis measured an effect and ran a procedure at the Vienna General Hospital in 1847, ordering examiners to scrub in chlorinated lime, and maternal mortality fell sharply and stayed down. The What remained "cadaverous particles". The effect was real and the procedure repeatable, and it still could not be transferred or defended, because the thing being acted on had not been individuated. That waited on germ theory.

## 4. Tools

Every change on every body requires force. Force always comes from a tool, and tools are the input most often treated as though it were free.

### 4.1 Force Comes From a Tool

Muscle moves matter. An operation moves a calculation. Attention and memory move an information state. Regulation moves an emotional state. Each is a made thing with a finite capacity, produced by prior states.

So every change is gated twice: once by the continuity of the path, and once by the strength of the tool that has to drive it. A path can be perfectly continuous and still not be walked.

This is a distinct way of being stuck, and neither missing material nor missing knowledge describes it. The direction is right, the target is individuated, the route is continuous, and there is not enough strength to cover it.

### 4.2 Self-Developing Tools

Some tools are built by being used.

A muscle is made stronger by use, and using it requires the muscle. So it can only develop from wherever it already is, in increments it can currently sustain. The same holds for an operation practised into fluency, for attention, and for emotional regulation.

This is the rule of section 3 applied to the tool rather than to the thing the tool moves. No jump from weak to strong. Every intermediate state occupied. Force applied at each step, drawn from the tool as it currently stands.

You cannot train from a strength you do not have, any more than you can build from material you do not have.

### 4.3 Strength on Each Body

Each of the seven bodies has a tool, and each tool has a strength that was built or left unbuilt.

This makes a state readable in a way that listing its contents does not. A state is not only open or closed on a body. It is strong or weak on it, and weakness is a different problem from absence, because weakness can be trained and absence has to be supplied.

A man cannot use his forgiveness arm if it is weak.

### 4.4 A Tool Applied to the Wrong Body

Because tools are body-specific, a strong tool can be brought to a problem it cannot touch.

Semmelweis is the case. His procedural tool was excellent: he designed a controlled intervention, enforced it, and measured the result. The weakness was on the possible body, where the entity had not been individuated. A stronger procedure could not fix that, because procedure is a logical-body tool and the gap was elsewhere.

This failure looks like effort rather than error, which is why it persists. Force is applied, sometimes a great deal of it, along the body where the actor happens to be strong rather than the one where the state is shut.

### 4.5 Tools Are Lost

Tools do not only fail to be built. They are lost, and a lost tool is not recovered by wanting it.

Dollo's law describes this biologically. Once the developmental pathway underlying a complex trait is gone, the trait does not return in its original form. The lineage did not decide against it. The tool was discarded under conditions that made it unnecessary, and rebuilding it would require translating back along a route for which no force now exists.

The same holds for operations that fall out of practice and institutions that lose the ability to do what they once did routinely. The record of a capability is not the capability.

## 5. Precedence in Practice

Progress is not a series of clever ideas. It is the assembly of prerequisites.

### 5.1 Bronze Before Iron

Historians sometimes ask why humanity spent over a thousand years in a Bronze Age, given that iron ore is far more abundant in the Earth's crust than copper and tin.

The usual answer, that iron needs a hotter fire, is wrong.

Copper melts at 1085°C and bronze alloys lower still, so bronze is a melt-and-cast technology. The metal becomes liquid and is poured, and moulds, pouring and finishing follow from that.

Iron melts at 1538°C. Ancient furnaces topped out around 1100 to 1200°C and could not reach it, so iron was never melted at all in this period. A bloomery reduces iron ore in the solid state, well below its melting point, producing a spongy bloom mixed with slag, and the slag must liquefy at around 1200°C to keep the reduced iron protected and the process moving.

So the barrier was not a temperature. It was that the accessible temperature ruled melting out entirely, which forced a different class of operation: solid-state reduction, repeated forging to consolidate the bloom and expel slag, then carburising, quenching and tempering.

The missing input was a tool, not a material. And the temperature gap between casting copper and running a bloomery is around a hundred degrees, which is not the difficulty. What centuries of bronze casting built was the furnace practice itself: refractory linings that survive long runs, tuyeres admitting air where it is wanted, bellows and draught control, and charcoal made reliably at scale and close to pure carbon, which supplies the reducing atmosphere that strips oxygen off the ore.

None of those is a temperature, and none could be skipped.

### 5.2 Newton Before Einstein

Newton's mechanics was not a flawed attempt at General Relativity. It was the outer limit of what could be observed and computed in 1687.

Einstein could not have published in 1687 because the inputs had not been assembled.

Le Verrier's 1859 calculation of Mercury's perihelion shift, which he put at roughly 38 arcseconds per century and Newcomb later revised to about 43, required a century and a half of telescopic observation recorded after Newton.

The 1887 Michelson-Morley experiment required an interferometer sensitive enough to detect nothing. The null result is the finding; the constancy of light speed is what was inferred from it.

Maxwell's 1865 field equations unified electricity and magnetism.

Einstein's physics was assembled out of the precise contradictions that Newtonian mechanics took two centuries to produce.

### 5.3 Carnivory

Carnivores did not choose meat in any deliberate sense.

Cats cannot synthesise taurine in sufficient quantity and must take it from diet. Feeding cysteinesulfinic acid as a putative precursor produces no significant increase in taurine in plasma, whole blood or muscle. They cannot make arginine. They cannot convert beta-carotene to vitamin A and so require retinol from meat.

Choice here means accumulated causal commitment. A lineage specialised around available fuel, and once specialised the route back closed. This is section 3 running over generations and tool loss in the sense of 4.5: no lineage jumps between dietary states, and the reverse translation requires force that no longer exists in the genome.

## 6. The Origin State

Every causal chain traced backward reaches a state with nothing before it. This is not one application of the rule among others. It is the only state with no predecessor, and everything else in this paper is downstream of it.

### 6.1 Information Before Light

Before the physical universe exists, what is present is informational potential. This is the uncaused term, and 6.2 derives why one is required.

By the rule, whatever is made first must be assembled from what is already present. Since information is the only available material, the first thing made is light.

Light is information.

A source of light is a source of information other than the origin, and can only appear downstream. A source of a thing cannot precede the thing it is a source of.

This is not an arbitrary narrative. It is the only ordering the rule permits given what that state contains, and it requires nothing about the physical nature of light at the origin.

The transition from formless potential to structured reality took the span it took because each stage had to generate the material of the next.

### 6.2 The Uncaused Term

Every made thing takes from what already is. So the sequence either runs back without end, or it terminates in something that makes without taking.

Only something uncaused, depending on nothing before itself, could make without taking. Everything else has to take, because everything else is made of what came before it.

This follows from the rule rather than being assumed by it. The slot is required. What fills it is a separate question, and every result here holds whichever way that question is answered.

### 6.3 Taking Is the Price of Being Finite

Critiques of existence often demand a world where nothing costs anything to anyone. In a finite reality this is impossible.

Life consumes other organised systems to resist entropy. A plant uses sunlight, minerals and water thereby excluded from competing plants. Simply existing as a physical object occupies space nothing else can occupy.

Taking is not a design flaw. It is the cost of being a distinct, finite thing in time, and the price of anything besides the uncaused term existing separately at all.

### 6.4 Taking Is Not One Quantity

That taking is unavoidable does not make all taking equivalent. Two properties of what is taken decide where a particular taking sits.

**Renewability.** Whether the resource replenishes within the timeframe that matters.

**Rarity.** How scarce it is relative to what the local state needs to remain viable.

Taking from something abundant and fast-regrowing and taking from something scarce and slow-regrowing are different acts, though both are literally taking. The first leaves the next state roughly intact. The second narrows it.

Mapped onto the two-axis measure, where υ tracks who an act benefits and ψ tracks what the energy is doing, taking does not sit at the destructive end of ψ merely because it is unavoidable. Where it sits depends on whether the take outruns what can regrow, given how scarce the thing actually is there.

### 6.5 The Universe as Locus

To all effect the universe acts like a wand. A locus. A focus where everyone's infinite potential and God's can be gathered and expressed in any form of order at all.

The wand is itself of the universe, so it is limited like every other tool. It gathers and focuses what is there. It supplies nothing from outside.

Man does the same through simulations, making universes inside the universe with no rules except self-imposed ones. That is man copying the act that made him. And he does not build one on a whim. He builds it for reasons, and they are the same reasons the universe was made, because the reason is part of what is being copied.

## 7. Human Action

### 7.1 The Actor Index

A causal state cannot be indexed to a historical era. Speaking of what was "possible in 1838" bundles unrelated people into one imaginary option set.

A government official drafting colonial policy in London and a frontier pastoralist facing immediate survival pressure shared no materials, no immediate information, no incentives and no tools. Claiming a choice was "available in 1838" is an error. A state must always be indexed to the actor at their exact coordinates.

### 7.2 Intention and Conditioning

Actors decide on three bounded parameters: the information they hold, the conditioning and worldview installed by their history, and their incentive structure balancing self-benefit against group- or other-benefit.

In high-scarcity, high-threat environments, survival conditions actors toward self-preservation or immediate tribal interest. In stable, surplus-rich environments, actors gain the room to consider broader outcomes.

In colonial Australia, expansion was the systemic outcome of exploration, trade patterns and imperial directive. Within that movement, factions formed: some acting for narrow self-interest, some aiming to establish order for the broader commonwealth, others holding varying degrees of altruism or hostility toward indigenous populations.

Every actor acted on what seemed viable, necessary or advantageous given their specific conditioning and the information they held.

## 8. Hindsight and Forgiveness

A common confusion is that recognising causal necessity excuses harm. It does not. It describes mechanics.

### 8.1 Necessity Is Not an Excuse

One can argue in hindsight that an actor should have done infinite other things in infinite other ways. They did not, because they were acting on, and acted upon by, the exact internal and external information present at that moment.

An excuse tries to absolve by pointing at mitigating factors. BCS does not absolve, praise or condemn. It describes the chain.

A wildfire that burns a town is causally necessary given heat, dry timber, wind and a spark. Saying so is not an excuse for the fire and does not make the destruction desirable. A destructive historical act is likewise the exact output of the actors' conditioning, information and pressures.

There is also a structural reason it cannot serve as an excuse. An excuse is offered from the present, about an act being performed or about to be. BCS applies to completed states. It does not reach the present, so by the time the claim holds, the state has closed and there is nothing left to excuse.

### 8.2 The Hindsight Spectrum

In hindsight an entire spectrum of choices becomes visible that was not accessible in real time. The four tiers are distinguished by tools, which makes the boundaries exact rather than matters of degree.

**The Best Impossible Action.** No tool for it exists anywhere, and none could, because it would require laws, energy, materials or mechanisms not present in the universe.

**The Best Improbable Action.** The tool exists somewhere, but this actor's was too weak to drive the translation. Survival threat, conditioning, panic and social pressure are not separate explanations. They are descriptions of a tool operating at or beyond capacity.

**The Best Possible Action.** The actor's tool was strong enough, on the body where the state was actually constrained.

**The Best Certain Action.** What the tool actually did. The single resultant determined by the exact materials, tool strengths, conditioning and information present at that second.

Looking backward, it is obvious that alternatives were theoretically available, and we naturally agree the actor should have chosen the one leading to the most good. But asserting they could have made that leap without the necessary information, tool strength or emotional capacity asks for an effect without its cause, and for a jump with no force behind it.

The line between Improbable and Possible is where nearly all retrospective judgment goes wrong, and it is not a moral line. It is a measurement of a specific capacity in a specific person at a specific moment, and it is not readable from the outcome.

### 8.3 Forgiveness

The spectrum establishes why forgiveness exists as a mechanical necessity, and where its boundary lies.

Hindsight reveals the actions that would have produced the most good, so the damage is clearly identifiable. Yet the actor was bound by their state. They could not have executed the best possible action, because the necessary information, tool strength or conditioning did not exist in them at that moment. Demanding the past have unfolded differently demands a world that never existed.

**Why anything can be forgiven.** Forgiveness is the recognition that a completed state cannot be altered. It releases the demand for an unpayable debt. There is no category of past action inherently unforgivable, because holding a grievance against a locked state is an argument against reality itself.

**Whether a thing can be forgiven and whether a person can presently forgive it are two questions.** The first is structural and settled above. The second is a matter of strength. Forgiveness is worked on the emotive body, where by section 3 states cannot jump and must translate under applied force, and by 4.1 that force comes from a tool with finite capacity. Understanding does not supply it. Understanding is a logical-body instrument, and applying it harder to an emotive-body constraint is the misapplication of 4.4.

A person can accept every step of this argument, agree the past state was locked, and still be unable to perform the forgiving, because that arm has not been built. That is not a failure of reasoning and it is not refusal. It is an undeveloped tool, and like any other it is built by use, from wherever it currently is, under force over time.

**Why refusal to recognise being forgiven cannot be forgiven.** There is exactly one boundary and it is definitional. Forgiveness is not a unilateral decree in empty space. It is the clearing of an offense between conscious actors. If someone refuses to recognise the harm, denies the need for reconciliation, or rejects the forgiveness offered, the act cannot take place.

You cannot forgive someone who refuses to accept being forgiven. This is not a penalty. It is what the word means. An offer that is rejected cannot be consummated, so the actor remains unforgiven by definition.

## 9. Suffering

### 9.1 The Definition

Suffering is acting with a lack of understanding.

Pain is an incoming sensory signal sent when tissue is damaged. Pain lands from the outside.

Suffering is located in the action. It is the friction, error, unintended harm and downstream wreckage generated when an actor acts inside an incomplete information state. It is never located in whoever it lands on. Pain lands. Suffering is done.

### 9.2 Why It Is Built Into Time

An actor's information is bounded by the history accumulated up to that moment. No actor in the present can hold knowledge that only later states will produce.

All action occurs in the present, and the present is by definition the position where the state is incomplete. So every action is launched from a partial state, and every action carries blind spots, miscalculations and unintended effects.

Suffering is not cosmic malice or bad luck. It is the structural consequence of living inside a sequence where knowledge must be earned step by step.

### 9.3 It Is Symmetrical

Two parties can each be aiming at what they take to be good, each acting on the best information available to them, and harm is produced by both, with nothing overriding anywhere in the chain.

That is the ordinary case, not the tragic exception. Where it looks as though one party aims at good and the other at bad, that reading is usually an artefact of a partial information state held by whoever is doing the reading.

### 9.4 The Tool Question

The better question is whether a tool can be made that cannot be used to create suffering in others. Since the universe is that locus, the question is about the universe.

The answer follows from the definition. The tool never creates suffering, because the tool has no understanding to lack. Only the one holding it does. There is nothing that could be built into the wand to fix this, because the thing that produces suffering was never in the wand.

Understanding is itself a tool, built by use from wherever it already is. So the gap between the understanding a person holds and the understanding an act requires is where suffering is produced, and it cannot be closed by being told to close it.

## 10. Mistakes and Regret

### 10.1 A Mistake

A mistake is an objective error located inside a single completed state.

When Le Verrier proposed Vulcan he made an empirical mistake in the What. His model did not match the universe. Identifying that requires no alternate reality. It is demonstrated by comparing his prediction against the actual sky.

A mistake is closed, inspectable and informative.

### 10.2 Regret

Regret is not an empirical evaluation. It is an impossible comparison.

Regret claims: given the exact same situation, I should have acted differently.

Returning to the exact same situation means the same tired brain, the same missing knowledge, the same emotional pressures, the same conditioning and the same tool strengths. Given that identical configuration, the choice could not have been different.

Regret judges a past decision using knowledge acquired after it, knowledge that did not exist in the actor's mind at the moment of choice. It demands two contradictory states at once.

It is also misdirected work. By section 9, regret is an act launched from a partial state, which makes it a producer of the very thing it is reacting to.

### 10.3 Correction

The only functional response to an error is correction.

Regret stares backward at a completed state that cannot be reopened. Correction takes that state, mistakes included, and uses it as material for the one being assembled now.

When an actor recognises an error, that recognition is new information. The actor takes it and uses it as stock to assemble a better action now. The past error stops being an unpayable debt and becomes fuel.

Regret treats a completed state as though it were still open. Correction treats it as stock.
