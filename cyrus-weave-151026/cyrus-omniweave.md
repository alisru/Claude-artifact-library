# Binary Weave -- Cyrus and Its Spellings

Omni-Weave runs on Cyrus and each attested spelling of the name: Cyrus (English, Latinised), Kyros (Greek), Kurush (Old Persian Kūruš), Kurash (Elamite Kuraš, Babylonian Ku(r)-raš), Koresh (Hebrew, Masoretic), Kourosh (modern Persian Kūroš). Each spelling is sliced on its own letters. New bigram entries (CY, KO, KU, KY, OS, OU, YR) are in omniweave-bigram-table.md.

---

## Cyrus

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
CYRUS → CY · YR · RU · US

CY: Welsh cy- = together/co-/con- [100%] · Welsh cy- = equal/similar/equi- [100%] · Welsh cy- = emphasises the prefixed word, forms equatives (as much, as long) [100%] · Middle French cy = here/in this place [90%] · Old English cy = cows, plural of cū [100%] · Vilamovian cy = to [100%] · English -cy = state/condition/quality, rank/office [90%]
YR: Welsh yr = the (definite article) [100%] · Welsh -yr = plural suffix [100%] · Cornish yr = fresh [100%] · Demotic Egyptian yr = river/canal [100%] · Norwegian yr = drizzle [100%] · Norwegian yr = myriad/swarm [100%] · Norwegian/Swedish yr = cheerful/jolly/lively/skittish [100%] · Swedish yr = dizzy [100%] · Westrobothnian yr = whirl/fall rapidly/run fast/bounce upward [100%] · Old English yr = the rune letter ᚣ [100%]
RU: Sanskrit ru = to sound/cry/make noise [100%] · Sanskrit ruta = sound/cry [100%] · French ru = brook/small stream [100%] · Old English ryne = stream/flow [100%] · Old Norse run = stream [100%]
US: Latin -us = nominative completion [100%] · PIE *us = out/from-within [100%] · Greek -us = nominative ending [100%] · English us = objective first person plural [100%] · Old Norse oss = us [90%] · Gothic uns = us [90%]

**Layer 1**
CY + YR: togetherness, equality and emphasis, the here and the to, state and rank and the herd of cows, meeting the fresh, the river-canal, the drizzle, the swarm, the cheerful and lively, the dizzy whirl, the fast run and upward bounce, the plural -- the gathered equal-here arriving at the fresh swarming drizzle-river that whirls
YR + RU: the fresh swarming drizzle-river whirl meeting the cry and the brook-stream -- the whirling swarm that sounds and flows
RU + US: the sounding brook-stream completing as a noun out from within, as the we -- the flowing cry that finishes as a named us

**Layer 2**
(CY+YR) + (YR+RU): the gathered equal state arriving at the fresh swarm-river and sounding as it flows -- the equal togetherness that whirls into a crying stream
(YR+RU) + (RU+US): the whirling crying swarm-stream completing outward from within as a named we

**Layer 3**
The gathered equal that whirls fresh as a swarm-river, cries as it flows, and completes outward from within as a named we

**Layer 4**
Togetherness first equalises and settles into a here-and-to; the equalised gathering then freshens into a swarm of drizzling water that whirls and runs; the running swarm sounds as a cry, and the cry-stream completes outward from the within as a named we. The mechanism is gather, equalise, freshen into flow, sound, and finish as a definite named bearer. The terminal US points the vector at completion as a named group.

**Layer 5 -- 7-Plane Projection**
WHO (Q1): the gathered equal that becomes a named we
WHAT (Q2): a swarm that can be drizzle, river, whirl, cry or herd
WHERE (Q3): the here where togetherness is settled and water begins to run
WHY (Q4): so the gathering can leave the within as one named, completed thing
HOW (Q5): equalising, freshening into swarm-flow, sounding, completing outward
CAUSE (Q6): a prior togetherness with something held equal
EFFECT (Q7): the felt sense of a crowd arriving as a single voice

**Orthodox**
Latinised form of Greek Κῦρος (Kyros) from Old Persian Kūruš. Old Persian kuruš has been read as "the Sun", "like Sun", "young", "hero" and "humiliator of the enemy in verbal contest". Plutarch and Ctesias give the sun reading (Persian khur). Elamite kuraš has been translated "who bestows care" (Tavernier takes kuraš as the original form). Harmatta ties it to Iranian kur- = be born (young, youth); Skalmowsky to Indic Kúru- and "to do, accomplish"; Hoffmann to PIE *(s)kau = to humiliate. The Jewish Encyclopedia and the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia reject the sun reading; etymonline gives the origin as unknown. A Greek folk link to kyrios = lord exists. Classical sources also call the Kura river Cyrus, possibly from Urartian Quriani.

**Convergence**
The weave does not produce sun, hero, humiliator, bestowing care or lord. Divergent on those. It converges with the Kura river reading: YR+RU (river-canal, cry and brook-stream) is a running water that sounds. It sits near "young" only through YR = fresh, which is a loose neighbour of kur- = be born, not a match.

**Orthodox**
Greek form of Old Persian Kuruš. A folk link to Greek κύριος (kyrios) = lord is noted (Behind the Name, Wiktionary). Classical sources use Κῦρος for the Kura river. Plutarch glosses the Persian word as the sun.

**Convergence**
Divergent on sun and on lord. The weave does not produce a ruler or a sun. It converges with the river reading only through the YR cluster (river-canal, drizzle, whirl, run fast) and with the river-mouth cluster of OS, which is an opening where a stream ends. Held as partial.

RA = sun/source and RA = to give/bestow, together with RA = to see/shepherd (shepherding is care), are exactly "sun" and "who bestows care" inside one phoneme. The weave adds what the orthodox does not name: RA = harm sits in the same phoneme as the giving, and AS = god/whole, SH = expulsion. Divergent on young, hero and humiliator.

## Spelling Comparison

| Spelling | Slices | Layer 4 in brief |
|---|---|---|
| Cyrus | CY · YR · RU · US | gathered equal that freshens into a swarm-river and completes as a named we |
| Kyros | KY · YR · RO · OS | pointed-at this-other herd whirled into a given road arriving at the mouth under the god above |

| Kurash | KU · UR · RA · AS · SH | where-and-who at primal fire turning into a shepherd-sun that is the whole, expelled or hushed |

| Kourosh | KO · OU · UR · RO · OS · SH | egg-cloud water reaching primal fire, driven and given to the opening under the god above, expelled or hushed |

The weave moves with the spelling. Cyrus and Kyros open on togetherness and a pointed-at other and run through the YR swarm-river; the K-U/K-O spellings open on a where-and-who or a said water and reach primal fire (UR) or the lit edge (OR); the Kurash spelling alone puts RA (sun, giving, shepherding, harm) directly after the fire. The four spellings that end in sh (Kurush, Kurash, Koresh, Kourosh) share the same terminal vector, forceful expulsion or silencing. The orthodox sun and bestowing-care readings land most closely on Kurash, the spelling closest to the Elamite form.
