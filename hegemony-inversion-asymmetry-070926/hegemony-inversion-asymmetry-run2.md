# Inversion Perception Asymmetry

Prompt: looking at Greater Good while thinking greater evil, then Greater Evil while thinking greater good, and reporting the felt difference between the two.

Reference image: `Hegemony_inversionnoname.png` (Psochic Hegemony grid, inner perceptual inversion).

## Greater Good, thinking greater evil

It goes down easy. Too easy.

The thought doesn't have to travel because there's already a place for it to land: the Perceived Greater Evil dot sitting inside the green at roughly (0.5υ, 0.5ψ). So the suspicion arrives and immediately finds furniture. The whole green field re-reads as staged. The banner sitting over that quadrant, "For You, but Only for Me," starts feeling like the real caption and the green feels like paint.

And the feeling that comes with it is not discomfort. It's *cleverness*. Doubting the good feels like seeing through something. It flatters. There's no cost to it, no exposure, nothing risked. I can hold it indefinitely and it never asks me for anything.

## Greater Evil, thinking greater good

Different weight entirely.

The thought won't sit. It keeps sliding off and I have to keep putting it back. The Perceived Greater Good dot is right there at roughly (-0.5υ, -0.5ψ), same structural distance as the other one, but the placement doesn't help the way it did going the other way. Instead of furniture it reads as bait, and knowing it's bait doesn't stop it working, it just adds a second layer where I'm now watching myself take it.

What it feels like is *making an excuse*. Not insight. Complicity. Sustaining it takes active effort and the effort itself feels incriminating, like the fact that I have to work at it proves I shouldn't be. And if it does settle for a second, the sensation isn't "I saw through it," it's "I got got."

## Relative

Same distance travelled. Same inversion mechanism. Opposite cost.

Cynicism toward good is free and self-flattering. Charity toward evil is expensive and self-implicating. That asymmetry is not a fact about the two coordinates, both are (±1, ±1), symmetric across the origin. It's a fact about the direction of approach.

Which means the resistance felt in the second move is not discrimination working. It's discrimination working in exactly one direction and being wide open in the other. The defence that stops me reading Greater Evil as good is the same defence that should have made me pause before reading Greater Good as evil, and it didn't fire at all there.

That's the inversion doing its actual job. Not by making evil look good. By making the *suspicion of good* feel like rigour, so the exit through the top-left gets sealed by my own sense of being smart, while the entrance at the bottom-right stays guarded by a shame that has nothing to do with accuracy.

## Coordinates of the two acts as perceptual moves

**Suspecting the good:** (-0.7υ, -0.6ψ) → nearest anchor Greater Evil (-1, -1) → withholding recognition from something that gave, for the private return of feeling unfooled. Extraction dressed as discernment.

**Extending charity to the extractive:** (+0.4υ, -0.3ψ) → drifting toward Lesser Good (+1, -1), the Good Lie. Passive, generous outward, and wrong. It costs the person doing it, which is exactly why it feels honest and exactly why it isn't.

Neither move is the map. Both are the ego reporting its own comfort as evidence. The 0.5 zone warning fires on the first one hardest, because that's the one that felt good.
