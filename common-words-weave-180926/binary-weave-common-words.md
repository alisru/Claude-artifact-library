# Binary Weave -- Common Words Test

Omni-Weave runs on common nouns rather than names, testing whether convergence behaviour differs between basic vocabulary (rain, fire) and constructed vocabulary (umbrella), and what the correct move is when a compound diverges from its source morpheme.

---

## Rain

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
RAIN → RA · AI · IN

RA: Hebrew ra (רַע) = evil/harm/bad [100%] · Egyptian Ra = sun/source [100%] · Sanskrit ra = to give/bestow [100%] · Arabic ra = to see/shepherd [100%] · Japanese ra (裸) = naked/bare/uncovered [100%] · Old Norse rá = corner/rafter [100%]
AI: Japanese ai (愛) = love [100%] · Greek ai = interjection of pain/grief [100%] · Hebrew ai (אַי) = where/woe [100%] · Sanskrit ai = to go toward [90%]
IN: English in = within [100%] · Latin in = in/not [100%] · German in = in [100%] · Sanskrit in = master/one-who-possesses [100%] · PIE *en gives in [100%]

**Layer 1**
RA + AI: the harmful giver, the bare uncovered sun-source that sees and shepherds, sitting at its own corner, goes toward love and grief alike, toward the where of woe
AI + IN: the love and the grief that is the where-of-woe, going toward what is within and not without, entering into what possesses it there

**Layer 2**
(RA+AI) + (AI+IN): the harmful, bare, giving source that sees and shepherds from its own corner goes toward love and grief without distinction, and arrives now within -- not without -- entering into the possession of what receives it

**Layer 3**
The harmful giving source, bare and shepherding, goes toward love and woe alike and is taken within, into what possesses it

**Layer 4**
The uncovered source that is simultaneously harm and gift, that sees as a shepherd sees, releases toward love and toward woe without choosing between them -- and the release only completes once it has gone in, not remained without, and been taken into the possession of whatever receives it, at its own corner. Giver and harm are the same act; what arrives is claimed rather than given away.

**Orthodox**
Middle English rein, Old English regn, from Proto-Germanic *regna- (Old Saxon regan, Old Norse regn, Gothic rign), with no certain cognates outside Germanic -- unless (Watkins) from a presumed PIE *reg- = moist/wet, possible source of Latin rigare = to wet/moisten (behind irrigate), though de Vaan disputes the Germanic connection.

**Convergence**
The orthodox root is a Germanic isolate whose gloss is simply the referent, so there is no independent root meaning available to converge with. The one contested extension, *reg- = to moisten (rigare), operates on exactly the plane the cascade terminates in: IN, the within that possesses. Irrigation is the AI+IN movement made agricultural -- the going-toward that completes only by being taken inside. The RA split (giver and harm, held as a genuine non-converging dual in the table) is the referent's own dual, the same arrival being the crop and the flood -- and the Old Norse rá (corner/rafter) sitting inside that same slice is the angled place where the arriving water collects. Convergence here is with the referent's mechanism rather than with a root gloss, because no root gloss exists.

## Fire

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
FIRE → FI · IR · RE

FI: French fi = interjection of disdain/rejection [100%] · Esperanto fi = fie/shameful [100%] · Jamaican Creole fi = for/to [100%] · Welsh fi = me/I [100%] · Arabic fī (في) = in/at/within [95% -- long vowel]
IR: Spanish ir = to go [100%] · Portuguese ir = to go [100%] · Latvian ir = is/there-is [100%] · Lithuanian ir = and/also [100%] · Welsh ir = fresh/green/verdant [100%] · Danish ir = verdigris/copper-patina [100%]
RE: Latin re- = again/back [100%] · Hebrew re (רֵעַ) = friend/neighbor/another [100%] · musical re = second note of the scale [100%] · Old French re- = again/anew [100%] · Greek rheo = to flow [90%]

**Layer 1**
FI + IR: the shameful thing rejected, held for and toward the self within, goes and is -- and also, fresh and green, is what verdigris forms upon once it has been exposed
IR + RE: what goes and is, fresh at one edge and crusted with verdigris at the other, flows again and anew toward the neighbor beside it, the second one after the first

**Layer 2**
(FI+IR) + (IR+RE): the shameful thing rejected, held for the self within, that goes and is -- fresh and green at one edge, crusted with what it has already touched at the other -- flows again toward whatever stands beside it, the second one, and repeats from there

**Layer 3**
What is rejected from within goes and is, fresh at its leading edge and crusted at the trailing one, flowing again toward whatever stands beside it as the next one

**Layer 4**
What is held within -- disdained, cast toward the self and then away from it -- becomes a going-that-is: fresh and green at its leading edge, crusted with what it has already touched behind it, and it does not stop there but flows again, arriving at the neighbor, the second one, and repeating the whole motion from that new position. A structure whose entire existence is this repeated arriving-at-the-next, leaving its own residue at every point it has already reached.

**Orthodox**
Old English fyr, from Proto-Germanic *fūr-, from PIE root *paewr- = fire. PIE had two roots for fire: *paewr-, the inanimate root naming fire as a substance, and *egni- (source of Latin ignis, Sanskrit agni), the animate root naming it as a living force.

**Convergence**
Stronger than expected once every citation is carried through. The Hebrew re (neighbour, the one beside) turns the terminal into a movement toward what is adjacent, and adjacency-plus-repetition is the propagation behaviour of the referent specifically, not a generic property of moving things -- a river does not arrive at its neighbour and start again there. The Danish ir (verdigris, the crust that forms on what has been exposed) is the residue left behind; the Welsh ir (fresh/green) is the leading edge. Those two citations sitting inside the same bigram give both ends of the same event, and the Jamaican Creole fi (for/to) and Latvian/Lithuanian ir (is/and) both land on the same directional continuity: motion that is, going for and toward what comes next.

Divergent on substance: no bigram produces heat, light, or consuming, and that absence is held rather than repaired. What the cascade does produce -- a motion that must keep reaching the adjacent thing to continue -- is a process description matching the fact that the English word descends from the inanimate substance-process root (*paewr-) rather than the animate agent root (*egni-) PIE kept alongside it. The weave lands in the correct half of a split the language itself made.

## Umbrella

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
UMBRELLA → UM · MB · BR · RE · EL · LL · LA

UM: German um = around/about [100%] · Old Norse/Icelandic um = around/concerning [100%] · Portuguese um = one/a [100%] · Serbian/Croatian/Czech/Russian um (ум) = mind/intellect/skill [100%] · English um = hesitation-pause [100%]
MB: structural -- nasal-into-stop cluster, the hum closed into a hard stop; no standalone cross-language citation found
BR: onomatopoetic burr/vibration -- the rolled-R trembling as a standalone unit; the brr shiver as its felt consequence; no other standalone attestation
RE: Latin re- = again/back [100%] · Hebrew re (רֵעַ) = friend/neighbor/another [100%] · musical re = second note [100%] · Old French re- = again/anew [100%] · Greek rheo = to flow [90%]
EL: Hebrew El (אֵל) = God/the-strong-one [100%] · Phoenician/Aramaic ʾl = god [100%] · PIE *el = to flow/move [100%]
LL: structural -- intensifying doubling of the lateral; the flowing sound extended to completion
LA: French la = the (feminine definite) [100%] · Spanish la = the/her [100%] · Italian la = the/her [100%] · Arabic lā (لا) = no/not [95% -- long vowel] · Portuguese lá = there [100%] · Chinese lā (拉) = to pull/draw [100%] · Norwegian la = to let [100%] · solfège la = sixth note [100%]

**Layer 1**
UM + MB: the around, the one, the mind, and the hesitation-pause itself, all halted at the hard stop -- encirclement and thought sealed shut mid-breath
MB + BR: the sealed stop set shivering -- the hard closure trembling at what has been halted inside it
BR + RE: the shiver returns again and anew, flowing back toward the neighbor beside it, the second one repeating
RE + EL: the again-and-neighbor, the second one, flows and flows again toward the strong one, toward God
EL + LL: the strong flowing force, God-in-motion, doubled and extended to its full completion
LL + LA: the completed extension meets the definite one that is there, that says no, that pulls and draws, and that lets go -- the sixth note closing out the scale

**Layer 2**
(UM+MB) + (MB+BR): the around-one-mind-pause, halted at the hard stop, now trembling there -- the sealed thing shivering under its own closure
(MB+BR) + (BR+RE): the trembling halt returning again and anew, flowing toward the neighbor, the second one repeating past the stop
(BR+RE) + (RE+EL): the shiver-that-returns-toward-the-neighbor arrives now at the strong flowing force, at God-in-motion
(RE+EL) + (EL+LL): the flow-again-toward-the-strong-one is now doubled, extended to its fullest completion
(EL+LL) + (LL+LA): the strong flow extended to completion meets the definite one that is there, refuses, pulls, draws, and lets -- closing on the sixth note

**Layer 3**
The around-one-mind is halted and trembles there, and its shiver returns again and again toward the neighbor, arriving at the strong flowing force, which is doubled and extended to full completion -- and that completion meets the definite one that is there, refuses, pulls, draws, and lets go

**Layer 4**
The encircling one -- around, singular, minded -- is stopped short and held trembling there, and what shakes it is a force that keeps returning, reaching toward whatever stands beside it, building on each return until it is a strong current extended to its fullest extent; and the whole structure exists only to meet that current, at its far edge, with the definite refusal that is there -- the no that draws the current toward itself, pulls it in, and then lets it go. The stop, the tremble, and the built-up flow are one continuous mechanism whose entire purpose is the letting-go at the end.

**Orthodox**
Late Latin umbrella, altered (by influence of umbra) from Latin umbella = sunshade/parasol, diminutive of umbra = shade/shadow. Entered English in the early 17th century via Italian ombrella. The Latin names a sun-device; the Greeks introduced Europeans to the sunshade and the Romans extended it to rain.

**Convergence**
Divergent on the root: nothing in the cascade yields shade, shadow, or darkness. The sun-metaphor the Latin encodes is entirely absent.

Convergent on the object as it now functions: the around-one held rigid at the stop, trembling under a repeated current (EL = flow, doubled and extended by LL), refusing it at the far end (Arabic lā = no), pulling it in (Chinese lā = to pull/draw) and letting it run off (Norwegian la = to let). The weave reads the working device; the etymology preserves a fossil metaphor the object has outgrown. The terminal-vector hypothesis holds -- the final bigram aims the whole structure at refusal-pull-and-release, which is the edge where the device does its work.

## Umbra -- Source-Morpheme Run

Where a compound diverges from its orthodox root, the combinationary move is to descend one morphological level and weave the source morpheme itself: the compound's phonetics carry the assembled object, the morpheme's phonetics carry the original concept.

**Step 0 -- Phonetic Slices**
UMBRA → UM · MB · BR · RA

UM: German um = around/about [100%] · Old Norse/Icelandic um = around/concerning [100%] · Portuguese um = one/a [100%] · Serbian/Croatian/Czech/Russian um (ум) = mind/intellect/skill [100%] · English um = hesitation-pause [100%]
MB: structural -- nasal-into-stop cluster, the hum halted; no standalone citation
BR: onomatopoetic burr/vibration; the brr shiver
RA: Hebrew ra = evil/harm/bad [100%] · Egyptian Ra = sun/source [100%] · Sanskrit ra = to give/bestow [100%] · Arabic ra = to see/shepherd [100%] · Japanese ra = naked/bare/uncovered [100%] · Old Norse rá = corner/rafter [100%]

**Layer 1**
UM + MB: the around, the one, the mind, and the hesitation-pause, halted at the hard stop -- encirclement and thought sealed shut mid-breath
MB + BR: the sealed stop set shivering -- the hard closure trembling at what has been halted inside it
BR + RA: the shiver held against the harmful giving sun-source that sees and shepherds, bare and uncovered, gathered at its own corner

**Layer 2**
(UM+MB) + (MB+BR): the around-one-mind-pause, halted at the hard stop, now trembling there
(MB+BR) + (BR+RA): the trembling halt now at the harmful giving source -- bare, uncovered, seeing and shepherding -- gathered at its own corner

**Layer 3**
The around-one-mind is halted and trembles there, and what it trembles at is the harmful giving source -- bare, uncovered, seeing and shepherding -- gathered at its own corner

**Layer 4**
The encircling one comes to a stop and stays there shivering, and the whole of its condition is this: held at the bare giving source that is also harm, that sees as a shepherd sees, gathered at the angled corner where giver and wound are the same act. It has no content beyond the halt it makes there.

**Orthodox**
Latin umbra = shade/shadow. In English from the 1590s as phantom/ghost, the astronomical shadow-cone sense recorded by the 1670s.

**Convergence -- Partial**
The terminal bigram of the Latin word for shadow is RA, the source itself, and under the terminal-vector hypothesis the final bigram is where the force is aimed. The cascade produces a halt aimed at the giver, which is the harder half of the orthodox meaning, since the referent is a thing defined wholly by its relation to a light source and by nothing else. The BR shiver sits inside the word for the one place that stays cold. The Old Norse rá (corner/rafter, the angled edge) matches the geometry of the thing, which is a cone.

What the cascade does not produce is the absence. Nothing in UM·MB·BR·RA yields negation, blocking, or darkness. The weave gives the relation to the source and the coldness; the orthodox supplies the failure of light to arrive. Enough to show the descent move works -- compound-level divergence sends the run down one morphological level and convergence resumes -- but a partial hit, not a strong one.

## Findings -- Common Words vs Names

Three distinct convergence targets emerged from one method.

**Basic words with isolate or self-referential etymologies (rain).** The orthodox root glosses as the referent itself, so there is no independent root meaning to converge with, and convergence has to be measured against the referent's mechanism instead. Rain hit on mechanism: the disputed *reg- = to-moisten line operates on the IN plane the cascade terminates in, and the RA giver-harm dual is the referent's own crop-and-flood dual.

**Basic words where the cascade produces a process rather than a substance (fire).** No heat, light or consuming appears anywhere in the citations, and that absence is held. What the cascade does produce -- a motion that must keep arriving at the adjacent thing to continue -- is propagation, which is specific to the referent rather than generic to moving things, and it matches the inanimate substance-process root the English word actually descends from rather than the animate agent root PIE kept alongside it. The weave lands in the correct half of a split the language itself made.

**Constructed and compound words (umbrella).** The compound's cascade reads the assembled object's current function; the etymology preserves a fossil metaphor the object has outgrown. Where compound-level divergence appears, descend one morphological level and weave the source morpheme. Umbra converged partially -- terminal RA gives the relation to the source, the absence half is missing -- enough to support the descent move as the combinationary method for compounds without overstating what it recovered.

Working generalisation to test on further runs: names converge with orthodox etymology, common nouns converge with referent mechanism, compounds converge only after descent to the source morpheme.
