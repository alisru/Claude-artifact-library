## Combined DSP + NDIS, single person, average plan

- Nominal combined total: $31,223 (DSP) + $31,000 (NDIS avg) = **$62,223/yr**
- Post reform-target NDIS average (~$26,000): combined falls to **~$57,223/yr**
- Minimum wage full time gross: **$52,255/yr**

Nominal combined total currently exceeds minimum wage by roughly $10,000, narrowing to roughly $5,000 once the reform's plan-size target lands.

## The gating problem

Only the DSP portion ($31,223/yr) is liquid, unrestricted income the person directs themselves. The NDIS portion is not income, it is a hypothecated claims scheme: pre-assigned to approved categories, paid to providers, non-transferable between categories, and audited for use. It functions as insurance against a defined risk category, per the scheme's own name, not as a wage or income substitute, even though in practice it covers costs of daily living (mobility, personal care, therapy, equipment).

So relative to a full time minimum wage income, the actually available spending power (DSP alone) sits at roughly 60% of minimum wage. The remainder is real support but locked behind approval, provider markets, and now tightening reform criteria.

## Underspend / non-delivery

- Roughly **one third of all NDIS funds go unspent** in an average plan year (Plan Partners, e61 Institute). Since NDIA funding periods were introduced in May 2025, unspent funds roll over automatically between funding periods *within* the same plan (e.g. quarter to quarter). They do not roll over *between plans*: at plan reassessment or renewal, whatever is left unspent when the old plan ends is not carried into the new plan and returns to the NDIA.
- Disability sector submissions, notably the Australian Physiotherapy Association's 2025-26 NDIS Annual Pricing Review submission, argue this underspend is not evidence of excess and instead reflects provider shortages, rigid pricing rules, poor plan design, and access friction. This is the sector's stated interpretation, not an NDIA-confirmed causal breakdown, and is flagged here as such.
- On the $31,000 average plan, that is roughly **$10,000/yr per person allocated but never converted into delivered support**.
- Effective delivered NDIS support after accounting for underspend: roughly $31,000 - $10,000 = **~$21,000/yr actually reaching the person**.
- Effective combined total actually delivered (DSP + used NDIS): $31,223 + $21,000 = **~$52,223/yr**, which lands almost exactly at the minimum wage figure ($52,255/yr) rather than exceeding it.

This matters for the political framing: the headline "combined support exceeds minimum wage" argument relies on the full allocated NDIS figure, not the delivered figure. Once underspend (a structural access failure, not a surplus) is accounted for, the actually-received total roughly equals, not exceeds, a full time minimum wage, while carrying none of that wage's liquidity.

## Participants, total spend, and funding brackets

- Active participants: **774,456** as at 31 March 2026 (NDIA Quarterly Report Q3 2025-26).
- Actual payments FY2024-25: **$45.85 billion** across ~739,000 participants. Total committed (allocated) supports at 30 June 2025: **$61.02 billion**.
- Projected total scheme spend 2026-27: **~$56.1 billion** (Federal Budget Paper).
- National utilisation rate: **75%** (Oct 2024 to Mar 2025 measure).

### Two competing "average plan" figures, flagged as an unresolved discrepancy

- **Government/political framing figure**: Minister Butler's National Press Club speech (22 April 2026) and the subsequent reform announcement cite average plan spend as **$31,000** this year, targeted down to **$26,000**. This is the figure the earlier sections of this tracker use.
- **NDIA scheme-wide mean payment**: $45.85bn / 739,000 participants ≈ **$62,000/yr** (FY24-25 actual payments), and annualising the March 2026 quarterly payment figure gives a similar **~$64,000/yr**.

These two figures are roughly double one another and I have not found a published reconciliation. Best-supported inference (flagged as inference, not confirmed): <cite index="101-1">the NDIA's own reporting notes the average payment is much higher than the median because of a skewed distribution, a small number of participants receiving very high-cost supports and a large number receiving low-cost supports</cite>, so Butler's $31,000 figure is plausibly closer to a typical/median participant, while the NDIA's $62,000-64,000 is the full scheme mean pulled upward by a high-cost cohort. This has not been confirmed against an actual published median dollar figure.

### The cohort driving the concentration

- **SIL (Supported Independent Living)** participants: 36,808 people, $16.4bn/yr in SIL payments, averaging **~$447,100/yr each**. That is roughly 4.75% of participants.
- Grattan Institute: <cite index="65-1">nearly 40 per cent of the cost of the NDIS goes to about 7 per cent of people with the most intensive support needs.</cite>

### Effective income range by cohort, DSP + NDIS combined

| Cohort | NDIS allocated/yr | After ~75% utilisation | + DSP $31,223/yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical/majority participant (Butler's framing) | ~$26,000-31,000 | ~$19,500-23,250 delivered | **~$50,700-54,500 combined** |
| Scheme-wide mean (NDIA payment data, skewed) | ~$62,000-64,000 | ~$46,500-48,000 delivered | **~$77,700-79,200 combined** |
| SIL cohort (~4.75% of participants) | ~$447,100 | largely fully utilised (block-funded accommodation) | **~$478,300 combined** |

The typical-participant row lands close to the minimum wage figure ($52,255/yr) once underspend is factored in, consistent with the earlier "gating" analysis. The scheme-wide mean is inflated by the SIL/high-intensity cohort and does not represent what most participants receive.

## Reform trajectory to track

- NDIS average plan size target: down from ~$31,000 to ~$26,000 by 2030 (participant numbers targeted to ~600,000, growth slowed from 5 to 6% down to 2%).
- Social/community participation and capacity building budgets reset from 1 October 2026, aligned to 2023 spending levels (a real terms cut).
- Total scheme savings target: $37.8 billion over four years from the 2026-27 Budget.
- DSP indexed 20 March and 20 September each year against CPI/wages, minimum wage reviewed annually 1 July by the Fair Work Commission.
