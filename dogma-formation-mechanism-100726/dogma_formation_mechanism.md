## 2. The Entry Point Is Effect, Not Who

The conventional assumption is that enquiry begins with an agent, a Who, deciding to investigate a thing. This is backwards. The actual entry point into any dogma-forming sequence is Q7, Effect, the observed phenomenon itself. Effect is the only plane that requires no prior interpretation to register. A thing falls. That is witnessed directly, before any of the other six interrogatives have been asked of it.

Gravity, as the working example, begins here. Things fall down. That is the raw Q7 datum, the objective fact dogma will eventually either be built on correctly, or built on top of incorrectly.

This inverts the standard assumption embedded in ordinary reasoning, that Who comes first because a mind must exist to ask the question. Structurally, Who is not the starting plane. Who is the terminal resolution, the last interrogative answered, because the will or agent behind an effect is the least directly observable and most easily contaminated by premature assumption. This is consistent with the existing framework principle that Q1 cannot be declared upfront and must emerge only after Q2 through Q7 have been run. What this document adds is that Q7 is not merely one plane among six checked before Q1 closes, it is the fixed empirical anchor the entire enquiry opens from and is tested against.

## 3. The First Pass, Man Asking the Six Questions of the Effect

Once Q7 is registered, "things fall down," the six remaining interrogatives are asked of that effect, in principle simultaneously, though they can be walked in sequence for clarity.

Q2, What. What is the thing that is falling. What categories of object does this apply to.

Q3, Where. Where does the falling occur. Is it universal to all locations or bounded by some condition.

Q4, Why. Why does it fall. What is the falling in service of, what does it mean, what is it resonating toward.

Q5, How. How does it fall. What is the mechanism, the rate, the consistency of the behavior.

Q6, Cause. What causal or historical sequence produces the falling. What precedes it every time.

Q1, Who. Who or what is the agent or will responsible for the falling. This is resolved last, once the other five have supplied enough structure that an answer to Who is actually earned rather than assumed.

This is the first pass of the 7x7x7 structure at depth one, Q1 through Q7 run once against a single observed effect.

## 4. The Recursive Verification Pass

No answer produced in the first pass is accepted as settled on its own. Each of the six answers to Q2 through Q6, and eventually Q1, is itself re-run through all seven interrogatives again at the next fractal depth, using the +i operator, where i forms as the new Q step for a recursive depth drill of the 7x7x7 structure.

So the answer to Q4, why it falls, does not simply stand once given. It becomes its own Q1 through Q7 enquiry. Effect is asked of it again, then What, Where, Why, How, Cause, and Who, all applied to the why-answer itself, to verify that the answer holds structurally and is not simply a plausible sentence.

This recursive re-verification is what separates a resolved plane from an assumed one. A plane is only genuinely resolved when its own recursive pass closes, when asking the seven questions of the answer produces consistent, non-contradictory results rather than further unresolved gaps.

## 5. Where the Gap Opens

In practice, full recursive closure across all seven planes, at every depth, is rarely completed. Some question, at some depth, is not comprehensively answered. That unanswered space is not nothing. It is unknown potential, a genuine gap in the structure where the recursive verification did not close.

This unknown potential can only go one of two ways.

Kept open. The gap is acknowledged as unresolved and explicitly marked as such, left available for future interrogation. Nothing is asserted about it. The structure carries a visible hole rather than a filled one. In Hegemony Lens terms, this preserves the system's Q2 radius, because unexamined space remains visible as unexamined rather than being quietly absorbed into the map as if it had been surveyed.

Assumed in. The gap is filled with an answer that was never actually produced by a completed recursive interrogative pass. The answer is inserted, and from that point on treated as though it had been earned. Nothing distinguishes it, on the surface, from a plane that genuinely closed. It occupies the same slot in the structure and is cited with the same confidence.

That act of insertion, the assumed-in answer standing in for an unresolved recursive gap, is dogma. Orthodoxy is what dogma becomes once enough of these insertions accumulate across enough interrogative slots, get institutionalised, and get defended as a body rather than examined individually. This matches the existing lexicon mapping, where Doxa is unexamined opinion, Orthodoxia is doxa that has been institutionalised as the group's correct and settled view, and Dogma is doxa that has stopped moving entirely, fixed as permanent and no longer subject to revision.

## 6. Why This Is the Same Failure as Orthodox Bleed

This is structurally identical to a failure mode already identified inside the Binary Weave methodology, orthodox bleed, where the orthodox meaning of a whole word is seeded into the early slices and layers of an analysis instead of being earned by the cascade. Seeding the answer guarantees the cascade lands on the orthodox conclusion regardless of what the actual phonetic or structural evidence would have produced.

Dogma formation is the same error operating at the level of the seven interrogative planes themselves, rather than at the level of bigram slices. An unverified plane-answer is planted in a slot where a genuinely resolved or genuinely open answer belongs. Everything built afterward, every institution, doctrine, or claim that treats that plane as settled, inherits a debt it never paid down. The structure above the insertion looks sound. The insertion itself was never checked.

---

## 7. Why Orthodoxy Is Wrong

This does not mean every orthodox conclusion is false as a matter of content. Some assumed-in answers may happen to coincide with what a completed recursive pass would have produced. The wrongness is not primarily in the outputs. The wrongness is structural, in the method.

Orthodoxy, as a system, necessarily contains assumed-in answers presented as though they were resolved ones, because full recursive closure across all seven planes, at every depth, across an entire body of doctrine, is a standard that is almost never actually met. Orthodoxy is what accumulates in the gap between the appearance of closure and the fact of it. It survives specifically because the assumed-in answer and the genuinely resolved answer are indistinguishable from the outside, once both are simply asserted with confidence.

This is also why orthodoxy tends to outlast the people who built it. Once an assumed-in answer is institutionalised, defending it no longer requires anyone to have done the recursive work, it only requires citing that the answer already exists and is settled. This is consistent with the existing observation that the most dangerous configuration is the inactive group, where the rule runs itself through convention and nobody is driving or accountable for whether the original interrogative gaps were ever actually closed.

The corrective is not to reject every orthodox conclusion outright. It is to treat every plane-answer currently held as settled as a candidate for the recursive pass it may never have received, and to mark honestly, at every depth, whether a given answer was kept open, or assumed in.
